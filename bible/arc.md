**55 chapters, five arcs, one complete story. Chapters are 26-28 pages.**

# ARC 1 — THE CONSULTATION (ch1-12)

**April to mid-October, their second year.**

**IN-state.** Nagi cuts heads illegally for family and regulars in a shop three stations from a
school where nobody says her name. Tōma has been a photograph for two years. They have never met.
The second chair has been empty for six years. The countdown in the back of the appointment book
reads fourteen.

**OUT-state.** He is in the book at an interval no haircut can justify. There is a brown envelope
of his money in the workbench drawer that has never been rung into the till. The rule exists and
has been said out loud. Yaeko knows. Takafumi has seen him and said nothing. And one classmate, by
accident, at 19:44 on a Sunday, now knows everything and will never say so.

## CH 1 — "IN PENCIL"

**Premise.** 19:41 at Barber Komori, and Nagi is alone in it: her father is at the barbers'
association meeting until nine and her grandmother is upstairs with the baseball on loud enough to
come through the ceiling. We watch her close a shop, properly and at length — twelve towels out of
the cabinet and into the washer, the blue disinfectant jar topped up, the blade tin counted and
the number written on the tape on the lid, chair one pumped down, the till counted twice because
the drawer opens on the second push and she has a trick for it she has never told her father. Cut
back six hours: a compressed sequence at Kōyō in which Nagi appears in eleven panels and nobody
says her name once — a register is read out rather than answered to, a girl asks her for an eraser
without looking up, a teacher counts heads instead of calling them, and she is through the door
ninety seconds after the bell and alone by minute four of the twenty-minute walk. Back at the
shop, 19:52, she eats a cold ¥90 korokke from Nikuya Hirai standing up in three bites, which is
why her mouth is full at 19:55 when the bell gives both notes. A boy in the Kōyō uniform, tie
already off, bag on the wrong shoulder, asks if it is too late; she looks at the clock, then at
his head, and says the chair is still warm. Two metres beyond the glass, lit from below by the
streetlamp, his own face is on the utility pole at three times life size, and she does not look at
it, because she has never once looked at it. He asks for something that is not the poster —
shorter at the sides, but not so it looks like he tried — and she puts her hands into his dry hair
and finds a counter-clockwise whorl set left of the crown that every chain-salon cut of his life
has fought, and she asks: **HOW SHORT?** Then the cut takes ten pages and is the most sensuous,
specific thing in the chapter and carries almost no emotional dialogue: the spray bottle, the wide
comb, the guide section, the sound of the shears through a panel held between two fingers, the
whorl left to do what it was going to do anyway, the clippers up the nape, the corner taken off
around the ear, the basin, the towel snapped once in the air. He falls asleep for four minutes at
the shampoo and wakes up mortified. At the till she has to ask his name to write it in the book.

**Domestic beat.** The closing ritual as a sequence with steps: twelve towels unrolled, shaken,
carried through the curtain to the washer in the back room; the kettle put on for upstairs,
because Yaeko wants tea at 20:30 and not before and not after; the korokke on its square of
translucent paper, gone cold on the counter since 18:00, eaten standing up in three bites with the
broom still in her other hand.

**Ground gained.** He stays four minutes past the finish because she has not taken the cape off,
and neither of them mentions the cape. He books the next one himself, out loud, before she offers.

**Outside beat.** None.

**Final page.** The appointment book open on the counter under the till light: three weeks
forward, one line in 2B pencil — 19:30, and a name she had to ask for. Beyond the glass, the
striped pole turning half a beat slow, and on the utility pole beside it the exact face that just
walked out, three times life size, and she still has not looked at it. Her thumb resting on the
graphite. She blows the eraser dust off the page.

## CH 2 — "GROWING OUT"

**Premise.** The three weeks between. Golden Week arrives and Barber Komori does fourteen heads a
day for a week with the door propped and the fan oscillating through its broken 140 degrees, and
we finally see Nagi work at volume: six heads in one afternoon at chair one while her father is at
the basin, no corrections, the regulars talking over her head about the ward office and the price
of eel. At school, mid-terms are announced and Nunokawa Koharu asks Nagi to help carry the class
noticeboard; Nagi does it in silence, is thanked, and does not answer, and does not learn her
name. Yaeko comes down the fourteen stairs at 07:30 every morning, stands in the shop doorway for
about ninety seconds, and goes back up, and nobody in the family knows what she is checking or has
ever asked. On the 15th, Takafumi says three sentences about a customer's cowlick that are the
closest thing to praise he has ever produced in his life, and Nagi hears all three and says
nothing. And in the back of the appointment book, on the last ruled page, a number is crossed off
and a smaller number is written under it — one panel, no caption, no explanation, and the writer
must not explain it. Then it is Thursday, and it is 19:30, and the bell gives both notes.

**Domestic beat.** Golden Week lunch, eaten in relays: rice from the cooker on the back-room
shelf, three bowls, pickles out of a Maruko tub whose lid has never sealed properly, eleven
minutes total, standing, with a customer already on the bench. Takafumi eats in ninety seconds and
is back through the curtain before Nagi has sat down, and she eats his last two mouthfuls off his
bowl because throwing rice away is not a thing that happens in this building.

**Ground gained.** She recognises her own cut coming through the door before she recognises the
boy wearing it — three weeks of growth, and the fringe falling off the whorl instead of fighting
it. She has never before seen three weeks of her own work walk back into a room.

**Outside beat.** None. (Golden Week is shop rhythm, not plot.)

**Final page.** 19:30. Two notes. Nagi already reaching for the cape before she looks up — and
then looking up. He sits, and before she can ask how short, he asks her a question instead,
looking at the top of his own head in the mirror: **"DOES IT ALWAYS DO THAT?"** The chapter ends
on his hand half-raised toward his own crown, and her face, and no answer.

## CH 3 — "NO CHARGE"

**Premise.** Mid-May, and the chapter opens inside the cut already in progress — clippers at the
nape, him talking, in a register nobody at Kōyō High School has ever heard out of Naruse Tōma. The
towel cabinet's rubber band has perished, so the cabinet is sitting at thirty-eight degrees
instead of sixty and there is a wait, and while he is on the bench waiting he does something
nobody has done in this shop in eleven years: he reads the wall of framed licences. He counts
three. He counts one empty frame. He asks, mildly, which one is hers. She could lie in four words.
Instead she tells him, standing behind the chair with a comb in her hand and both of them in the
mirror: she does not have one; she cannot have one for fourteen months; every single thing she has
done to his head is illegal; and it is not her licence at stake, it is her father's, and the
premises registration, and the ward hygiene approval, and the building they live in. She says it
flatly and completely and without one word of apology, because she has decided that if he is going
to go he should go now and go all the way. Then she tells him she cannot take his money. He asks
two questions and both of them are practical. She answers both. And then she finishes the cut,
because the cut is half done, and those six pages are the best in the chapter — no dialogue about
what was just said, only a comb, a guideline, and the corner taken off around the left ear.

**Domestic beat.** The towel done by hand: kettle in the back room, a folded towel dunked, wrung
out over the sink in two twists, carried through steaming, snapped once in the air — and it is too
hot and she does it again, and the second one is right. Also the sharps tin: the used #10 dropped
in, and the small hard sound it makes, which is the sound this series will use for the law from
here on.

**Ground gained.** His two questions: what happens to the shop if somebody reports it, and whether
her father knows she does this when he is out. No moral question, not one. That is the entire
relationship, delivered in two lines, in chapter 3.

**Outside beat.** None.

**Final page.** ¥3,800 on the counter: a thousand-yen note folded twice, a five-hundred coin,
three hundreds. Her hand flat on the till drawer that only opens on the second push, not pushing.
The pencil already in her other hand. And him at the door, one hand on the frame, asking:
**"TWO WEEKS OR THREE?"** The chapter ends before she answers.

## CH 4 — "NINETY SECONDS"

**Premise.** Late May, mid-term week: desks apart, bags at the front, the corridor timetable taped
up crooked. Naruse Tōma gets his first proper chapter at school and the register is exact —
immaculate manners, complete sentences, nothing given away, and four separate people asking him
for the answer to question six between periods, none of whom say his name as though it belongs to
a person. Yagisawa Gaku arrives and is instantly the best joke in the arc: failing maths,
serenely unbothered, and the only human being in the building who talks to Tōma as if he were one.
And Tōma does something he has not done in two years — he looks at faces. It takes him about
ninety seconds of scanning a crowded stairwell to find her: back row of a different class, by the
window, plain frames, hair scraped up off her neck. Getting to that corridor costs him an invented
errand, which he invents perfectly, and he is twelve metres away when the bell goes and she is
gone before he reaches the door. Meanwhile Nagi's exam week runs from inside: she is not a poor
student, she is an *efficient* one, she finishes early, and she does not check her answers, because
checking is correcting and she does not correct. Nunokawa Koharu puts a spare eraser on her desk
without being asked and Nagi still does not learn her name. On the walk home Tōma takes the
Nishi-guchi exit for no reason he could defend to anyone, stands under his own face for the first
time in a year, and finds he cannot remember what day of the week the photograph was taken.

**Domestic beat.** Dinner upstairs — the first time in the series we go up the fourteen stairs.
Three people at a low table, the television on, Yaeko's hands wrapped around a cup because they are
better warm, rice, Maruko pickles, and Takafumi saying exactly three sentences across the whole meal,
all three about a customer's hair. Afterwards Nagi washes up alone with the window open, and the
striped pole's motor is audible from the street below, half a beat slow.

**Ground gained.** Nothing at school. That is the point. What is gained is the physical fact of
twelve metres of corridor he cannot cross, and one panel in which Nagi sees him, registers it, and
keeps walking — with one line of thought the reader gets and he never will.

**Outside beat.** Mid-term exams.

**Final page.** 19:55 at the shop. Nagi at the door, lights already off in the back half, looking
out at the corner. The appointment is not tonight and she knows it is not tonight. She has known
all day that it is not tonight. She checks anyway, and then she checks the clock, and then she
locks the door.

## CH 5 — "WITH THE GRAIN"

**Premise.** Early June, and the appointment book has quietly moved to a fortnight without either
of them ever saying the word. Tonight he asks for the full service — ¥4,600, cut and shave — and
she tells him exactly what that includes before he agrees to it, and he agrees to all of it. The
chapter is that service in something close to real time and it is the most intimate thing in the
arc: the consultation, the paper strip, the cape shaken out with a crack, the cut, the neckline,
the lather warmed in a bowl with water off the kettle, and the **single-use #10 blade snapped in
fresh in front of him so he hears it** — at which point she tells him, without drama, that the
razor is the part that is not grey at all, and he says go on anyway. Then the face: with the grain
first, across it second, never against it on a face this young. Then the basin, his eyes shut, his
whole face unguarded, wearing an expression eight hundred people at Kōyō High School have never
seen on it. Then the hot towel, laid from the chin upward, pressed at the temples and the bridge
of the nose. And under the towel — face covered, eyes covered, nobody able to see him — he talks,
and the chapter gives him four bubbles across four panels, which is the maximum this series
permits, and what he says is not about feelings: it is about a photograph taken on a Tuesday
afternoon in October two years ago, and how many people have told him since that they know
exactly what he is like. Nagi says almost nothing. She adjusts the towel and presses the temples a
second time, six seconds, which is not a step on the price list.

**Domestic beat.** The lather: the bowl, the brush, the kettle, the exact water temperature tested
on the inside of her own wrist, the circles worked in from the jaw. And afterwards the sink — the
brush rinsed and stood bristle-down on the shelf to dry, the blade snapped out, the yellow sharps
tin, the sound.

**Ground gained.** She performs a step that is not on the price list and does not explain it. He
does not thank her for it, which is exactly how the reader knows he noticed.

**Outside beat.** None.

**Final page.** The money on the counter, ¥4,600, and Nagi carrying it through the curtain to the
workbench drawer, where a brown envelope is waiting that has never been rung into the till, and
where the grandmother's old fixed-blade straight razor lies beside it — the one that has not been
legal to use on a customer in years and is honed twice a year by somebody. She is looking at the
razor, not the money, when the fifth stair creaks upstairs. Then it creaks again, going up. It has
been quiet for a long time before it creaked.

## CH 6 — "WHAT THE BROOM IS FOR"

**Premise.** Mid-June, nine straight days of rain. The shop smells of wet wool and the back room;
the dehumidifier bucket goes to the sink at 14:00 and again at 19:00; the towel cabinet is losing
its fight with the damp and every towel in the ward smells faintly of somewhere else. Every head in
Hanazono-chō is misbehaving, so the shop is busy for the wrong reason, and Tōma is in the book
again twelve days after the last one, which neither of them mentions. That morning, at 07:41 on the
Higashi-guchi stair landing, Nagi sees a first-year girl standing still in a moving crowd in front
of the poster — and, for the first time in the series, follows the girl's eyes and looks at it
herself. One panel. Three seconds. Then she looks away and keeps walking. Later, in class, someone
says his name out loud in a conversation Nagi is standing in the middle of, and she has to arrange
her face, and she is bad at it, and Nunokawa Koharu notices and says nothing to anybody. That evening
Tōma mentions, mildly, mid-cut, that Yagisawa has asked him where he goes on Thursdays. The rule
gets made afterwards, over sweeping: two people, one broom, a grey drift of somebody else's hair,
neither of them looking up, **under fifteen words in total**, and no reason given by either of
them — because both of them already know the reason and, by chapter 6, so does the reader.

**Domestic beat.** Wet weather in a shop with a floor: newspaper laid down inside the door and
replaced twice; the umbrella stand built for nine and holding fourteen; and one forgotten umbrella
that has been in it since March with a name written on the handle in marker that everybody has
read and nobody has done anything about.

**Ground gained.** The rule. And then, thirty seconds later, something smaller and larger: she
asks him to hold the dustpan — which she has never asked another human being to do — and he holds
it at the wrong angle, and she corrects it with the side of her foot without looking up.

**Outside beat.** None. (The stair landing and the classroom are character, not plot.)

**Final page.** The shop lights going off one bank at a time. The two of them out under the edge
of the arcade roof in the rain, the striped pole turning half a beat slow above them, and the
utility pole two metres away with his face on it, wet, lit from below by the streetlamp on its
photocell. And Nagi looks at it. On purpose. For one full panel. And says nothing the reader gets
to hear.

## CH 7 — "Yae-chan"

**Premise.** The third Saturday of the month, which is the one day Tatami Ōgiya shuts at noon, which
is the only reason Hagiwara Kiyoshi can be in chair one at 08:00 sharp, which he has been for forty
years. He pays ¥3,000 with the senior discount and tries to leave ¥3,500 and fails, as always. The
chapter is a full working Saturday seen from the outside in: eighteen heads in the book, the door
propped, the fan, the radio on AM for the baseball, the floor swept between every single head — and
Nagi at chair one for four of those heads while her father is at the basin, which everyone in this
shop treats as completely normal and which the reader, since chapter 3, cannot. Kiyoshi calls Nagi
"Yae-chan" twice and neither Komori corrects him. He talks about tatami the way a craftsman talks
about anything: how you can tell a floor was laid by a man who was angry that week, how rush grass
turns a different colour in a room that gets afternoon sun, and how his hands have about three good
years left in them, which he states as a fact about hands and not as a sad thing. Then he looks at
chair two for a long moment and says one sentence about it, and it is the first real information
the reader has been given about why nobody sits in it. At 17:20 Tōma turns up with no appointment,
sees the shop full, and — the beat of the chapter — goes to the corner, picks up the broom, and
starts sweeping. Nobody tells him not to. Takafumi looks at him for three seconds and says nothing at
all.

**Domestic beat.** Saturday lunch in relays: onigiri made at 06:40 from the back-room cooker,
wrapped in cling film, three on a plate, tea that goes cold before anybody gets to it. And the
mid-afternoon job that is nobody's favourite and is always Nagi's — the lidded hair bin, carried out
to the alley, which on a Saturday in July is genuinely unpleasant and which she does without one
word of comment.

**Ground gained.** Takafumi sees Tōma and says nothing, which will sit in the reader like a stone for
sixteen chapters. And Tōma, holding the broom, gets the dustpan angle right on the first try —
because he watched, three weeks ago, and remembered.

**Outside beat.** Hagiwara Kiyoshi's third Saturday.

**Final page.** 20:05. Shutter half down, floor clean, radio off. At the top of the fourteen stairs,
Yaeko — who has come down five steps and no further — looking past both of them at the second chair
with the whole day's noise gone out of the room. Her hand is on the handrail. She never uses the
handrail. She says four words to nobody in particular and goes back up, and the reader does not get
to be certain who she meant them for.

## CH 8 — "ON THURSDAYS"

**Premise.** The last day of the first term, and Kōyō is a building full of sweat and paper. The
buzz-cut season has taken the entire baseball club, so a third of the class is suddenly two
centimetres of hair, and one of them says out loud, three desks from Naruse Tōma, that the barber by
the river does a ¥3,800 in eleven minutes and it is the best cut in the ward. Tōma's face does
absolutely nothing, which costs him something. Yagisawa Gaku is fully built here: the one person
who has never mentioned the poster except as a joke to Tōma's face, who is failing maths with total
equanimity, and who asks the direct question — where do you go on Thursdays — because he is the only
person in the building who notices anything about Tōma at all. Tōma lies. It is a small, competent,
complete-sentence lie, it works perfectly, and he feels it land in his own stomach and stay there.
That evening he comes to the shop out of schedule, without booking, and does not ask for a cut; he
sits on the vinyl bench with the eleven-year-old magazines and stays fifty minutes, and Nagi, who
has one head left and then the floor, lets him sit there and does not ask why. She cuts a
nine-year-old's fringe for ¥2,000 while he watches, and the kid twists round in the cape, points at
him, and asks if he is the poster, and he says no, and the kid believes him completely and goes back
to talking about beetles.

**Domestic beat.** Summer in a shop with no proper air conditioning: the fan that only oscillates
through 140 degrees, the salt tablets in a jar beside the till, condensation running down the towel
cabinet's glass — and the ice, which Takafumi buys at Kawase Seika-ten every single day in July and
puts in a bowl in front of the fan, which does not work, which he knows does not work, and which his
mother did before him.

**Ground gained.** He comes without booking and does not ask for anything, and she lets him stay.
That is the first evening in the series where the chair is not the reason.

**Outside beat.** End of first term.

**Final page.** 20:10. The two of them and one broom and a floor with a nine-year-old's fringe on
it. Nagi asks him the first direct question she has ever asked him, one bubble, without looking up:
**"DID HE BELIEVE YOU?"** The chapter ends on his hands going still on the broom handle.

## CH 9 — "A DAY OFF"

**Premise.** The fourteenth of August: the only whole day in the year the shutter stays down, and
not one of the three Komoris knows how to be inside a building that is not open. Takafumi spends the
morning stripping and re-seating the hot-towel cabinet's door seals, which is not a day off. Yaeko
does the twice-yearly job on chair two — the tin, the cloth, two-fingered circles, the extra pass
over the cracked seam on the right — and it takes forty minutes, and Nagi watches the whole of it
from the fourth stair, and neither of them says a single word, and the smell of the leather food
fills the shut shop for the rest of the day. Then the flat gets too small and Nagi goes out, walks
the Asagi river path from Kotori Bridge to the weir and back, and ends up in the Higashi-guchi
ticket hall at 11:00 on a public holiday, where a first-year in a Kōyō summer uniform is wiping the
acrylic in front of a poster with a folded cloth and stops dead when she realises she has been seen.
Momose Rurika, sixteen, calligraphy club. The six pages that follow are the warmest thing in arc 1:
Rurika knows that photograph in forensic detail — the collar, the fall of the light, the four
millimetres of hair over the right ear that have been identical for two years — and knows nothing
whatsoever about the person, and says so herself, cheerfully, without any embarrassment at all once
she gets going. She is not deluded. She is sixteen. And Nagi, who has looked at that photograph
exactly once in her life, stands in a ticket hall listening to a stranger describe a haircut she has
now put her hands into eight times, and says nothing. On the way home she does not take the river
path.

**Domestic beat.** The leather on chair two: the tin with the rusted rim, the cloth cut from an old
towel, the circles, the seam, and the fact that Yaeko does this job kneeling because standing that
long is finished for her, and that nobody in this family has ever offered to do it for her, because
offering would be saying something.

**Ground gained.** None between the leads — they do not meet in this chapter. What is gained is
Nagi discovering that she has something to protect and that it has edges; and the reader
discovering that there is a person on the other side of the poster too, and she is not a rival, and
she never will be.

**Outside beat.** Momose Rurika.

**Final page.** 20:00 on a day the shop never opened. The appointment book on the counter, open at
a page with nothing on it. Nagi at the till, doing nothing, in a dark shop. Outside, the
streetlamp's photocell clicks and the face on the utility pole becomes the only lit thing on the
corner. She turns the shop light off so that stays true. From the stairs, Yaeko says something
completely ordinary, and the chapter ends on it.

## CH 10 — "TEN DAYS"

**Premise.** The first of September and the heat has not broken. In the appointment book the last
four entries in his name read three weeks, two weeks, twelve days — and tonight, which is ten days
after the last one, and there is not ten days of hair on that head and both of them know it. This is
the chapter in which the shop's other people begin to see: Takafumi stands at the counter on a Tuesday
morning, turns the page back once, looks at it, and turns it forward again. A regular, mid-cut, asks
Nagi whether they are doing student discounts now, because that boy is in a lot. She says the price
out loud, correctly — ¥3,800 — and does not answer the question, and the regular lets it go, and the
reader does not. And Tōma, in the chair, does the thing that makes the chapter: he asks her to take
almost nothing off. Two millimetres. A nape tidy, ¥1,000, ten minutes. And she performs it at full
craft on a head that did not need it — the paper strip, the cape, the clippers, the corner, the
towel, the duster — and for the whole ten minutes neither of them looks at the other in the mirror,
which is the only place in this shop where you can look at somebody.

**Domestic beat.** The first sanma of the year, upstairs: the grill, the smoke alarm that goes off
every September and is beaten with a folded newspaper, the window opened onto the corner where the
pole's motor is audible — and Yaeko deboning a whole fish in about eleven seconds with hands that
have not been able to hold shears in six years, while Nagi watches her do it and says nothing.

**Ground gained.** He pays ¥1,000 for a service he did not need; she performs it as though it were
¥4,600; the brown envelope in the workbench drawer gets thicker. And at the door he uses her given
name once, by accident, and she does not react, and then, after the door has shut, she does.

**Outside beat.** Second term begins.

**Final page.** The book, the interval column, the 2B pencil hovering — and then Nagi writing the
next appointment herself, on a line he never asked for, before he has reached the corner. The
chapter ends on her handwriting on that line, and the shop door still swinging, and the fact that
now she is going to have to tell him.

## CH 11 — "OPEN TO THE PUBLIC"

**Premise.** The Kōyō culture festival. Day one is students only; day two is open, and half of
Hanazono-chō comes through the gate, including — and the chapter never once says this out loud —
three of Barber Komori's regulars. Nagi's class runs a café and puts her on the till, which she is
frankly superb at, and the class discovers for the first time that Komori Nagi is good at something;
Nunokawa Koharu is the one who says so, quietly, once, and Nagi has no idea what to do with it and does
what she always does, which is to be somewhere else inside ninety seconds. Tōma's class puts him at
the front of something, and the school — without asking anyone — has printed his cram-school
photograph on the programme's sponsor page, which he finds out from a stranger's mother who wants
him to sign it. Momose Rurika runs the calligraphy stall, writes people's names for ¥100, is
genuinely excellent at it, and goes scarlet and ruins a stroke when he walks past. Yagisawa runs
interference all afternoon without ever once admitting that is what he is doing, which is the
funniest and kindest thing in the arc. And at 15:40 the two leads are in the same corridor with
eleven people between them, and neither of them changes pace, and the reader — who by now knows
exactly what that costs — has to watch it happen at full length.

**Domestic beat.** The class café as an actual chore: the urn, the paper cups counted out in
fifties, the float in a biscuit tin, the sugar sachets that will not stay in the box — and Nagi
silently rebuilding the entire counter workflow in about four minutes, because she has run a till
since she was nine, and nobody asking her how she knew, and her not wanting them to.

**Ground gained.** Eleven metres, uncrossed, deliberately, by both of them. And then at 19:20 in an
empty shop neither of them mentions it, and the not-mentioning is the ground.

**Outside beat.** The culture festival.

**Final page.** The shop at 19:50, last head gone. On the counter, left behind by somebody, the
festival programme, folded open at the sponsor page. Nagi alone with it, looking at a photograph of
a face she has had her hands on twenty times. And this time she looks at it properly, for as long as
she wants. Then the bell, behind her. Both notes.

## CH 12 — "NOTHING HAPPENED."

**Premise.** The Sunday after the festival, October, the first evening of the year you can see your
breath at 20:00. A deliberately eventless chapter: thirteen heads on a Sunday, Takafumi at chair one
all day, Nagi on the floor, the basin and the till, and Tōma arriving at 18:30 with a ¥50 bag of
crusts from Pan no Mimi because it was ¥50 and he had ¥50. He stays to close, and the two of them do
the last hour together — the twelve towels, the disinfectant jar, the blades counted and the number
written on the tape, the floor, the till that opens on the second push — over a conversation about
nothing at all that is the warmest sequence in the arc. At 19:44, Nunokawa Koharu walks from the arcade
down to the river road on her way back from her aunt's, forty metres off Hanazono Ginza, and the
corner shop's window is lit and the blinds are not down, because they are never down. Inside it, a
boy whose face is on the pole outside is holding a dustpan at exactly the right angle while Komori
Nagi — whose name Koharu has said aloud precisely once in her life — sweeps into it. Koharu stops
for four seconds. She looks at the gold lettering, and at the pole, and at the two of them, and at
the fact that neither of them is talking and both of them are smiling. And she keeps walking. She
does not tell anyone, and she never will, and the reader knows all three of those things and the
leads know none of them.

**Domestic beat.** The crusts: a paper bag of bread ends eaten off the waiting bench at 19:10 with
the last customer's hair still on the floor, and Takafumi taking one on his way past without breaking
stride or looking at anybody — the first thing in the series he has ever accepted from Tōma.

**Ground gained.** Takafumi takes the bread. And Tōma is given the blade tin to count and log, which
is a job and not a favour, and the difference between those two things is the entire chapter.

**Outside beat.** Nunokawa Koharu, four seconds at the window. Nothing else, all chapter.

**Final page.** The street, from outside, looking in. The lit window with two figures in it. The
striped pole a half-beat slow. His face on the utility pole under the streetlamp. And Koharu's back,
three-quarters of the way to Nakabashi, not looking round. She takes her phone out of her pocket.
She looks at it. She puts it back.

# ARC 2 — THE GUIDELINE (ch13-23)

**Mid-October to 30 December, still their second year. The densest arc in the book: eleven
chapters across ten weeks, because by now they see each other every few days and the intervals
have collapsed.**

**IN-state.** Nunokawa Koharu knows and has told nobody. The interval in the book is under two weeks.
The envelope of his money in the workbench drawer is thick enough to feel. They are not together.

**OUT-state.** They are together (ch16), which changes nothing visible except one column of the
appointment book. The shop has been through the biggest week of the barbering year on four hands
instead of two. And Takafumi knows — all of it, the boy and the book and the heads — and his answer
is not anger, and the reader will not get to hear what it is until arc 3 opens.

**Locked milestones pinned:** **ch16** they get together, domestic, mid-chore, absurdly small.
**ch23** her father finds out; the hinge; not anger.

- **13.** Koharu says nothing at all and instead starts leaving the classroom at the same second as
  Nagi four days running, walks the first half of the route beside her without asking one question —
  and on the Saturday brings her seven-year-old brother into Barber Komori and pays the full ¥2,000
  at the till like anybody else.
- **14.** Akie books Tōma into the chain salon by Kita-guchi for a proper cut before a parents'
  interview; the result is technically flawless and completely wrong, fighting the whorl at the
  crown so the fringe stands off it — and Nagi repairs another professional's work in twenty minutes
  without saying one word about it, which is the hardest thing she does all year.
- **15.** Shichi-Go-San, the fifteenth of November: a five-year-old's first proper haircut, his
  mother filming and crying and apologising for crying, Nagi holding his head still with two
  fingers, Tōma down on the floor making him laugh at the exact second the shears move — and the
  first lock going into a paper envelope with the date on it, which is how Tōma learns there is a
  shoebox upstairs with fifty-odd years of them in it, and one with her name on.
- **16.** **[LOCKED — THEY GET TOGETHER.]** The first hard cold snap, a Tuesday, no customers
  between 14:00 and 17:00: the towel cabinet's thermostat has failed and all twelve towels have to
  be re-boiled and re-rolled by hand, and her hands are red and his hands are red, and she holds the
  next one out and he takes it and takes her hand with it and neither of them lets go — and the only
  thing either of them says out loud is about the towel.
- **17.** Nothing changes and everything does: he still books, she still writes it down, and the
  only difference visible anywhere in the chapter is that she has stopped writing the name — just
  the time, in the left column, in pencil, because she knows.
- **18.** Mid-December: the year-end notice goes up in the window, Ichirin's pine takes over half
  the arcade and the whole street smells of it, and Takafumi says the second of his three-sentence
  things — this one to Nagi, about the fact that the thirtieth will need four hands and he has two.
- **19.** The twenty-sixth of December, 07:40: the queue starts on the pavement before the shutter
  is up, the appointment book is shut and put in the drawer because for six days it is first come
  only, and the shop does thirty-one heads before Nagi has eaten anything at all.
- **20.** The twenty-eighth: fourteen hours on the floor — and Yaeko comes down all fourteen stairs
  at 09:00, does not sit in chair two, stands beside the towel cabinet handing out rolled towels for
  six hours, and goes back up without a word to anybody.
- **21.** The twenty-ninth: Tōma turns up at 06:50 with four hot cans from the machine outside
  Tomoshibi, works the door and the bench all day for nothing, learns twenty regulars' names, and is
  being called "the lad" by three of them before closing.
- **22.** The thirtieth, morning: Takafumi's right hand cramps at the eleventh head and he goes into
  the back room with the curtain open and drinks tea for nineteen minutes; Nagi takes two of his
  heads while he watches through the gap; and the reader finishes the shop's real arithmetic several
  pages before either Komori does.
- **23.** **[LOCKED — THE FATHER FINDS OUT.]** The thirtieth, 18:20, queue still out the door:
  Takafumi calls "next," and Tōma — on the step since four o'clock — says he will wait for Nagi-san.
  Takafumi looks at the queue, and at his daughter, and then takes the appointment book out of the
  drawer and turns back a year, to where one name becomes no name and just a time. He says nothing
  to anyone for forty minutes and then cuts the next head. What he does at 22:10, with the shutter
  down and the floor unswept, is the hinge of the entire series, and it is not anger.

# ARC 3 — AGAINST THE GROWTH (ch24-34)

**1 January to 31 March. The winter arc: everything gets run the wrong way up the nape to see what
is really there.**

**IN-state.** Takafumi knows. They are together and almost nobody has been told. The shop has just
proved, in six days and ¥730,000, that it runs on four hands.

**OUT-state.** Nagi cuts under conditions and keeps a written log with her grandmother's
corrections in the margin. The panel on the utility pole outside the window carries somebody else's
face. Naruse Tōma is nobody in this ward for the first time since he was fifteen, and it is the
best thing that has ever happened to him, and it has taken the floor out from under him. The second
year is over.

**Locked milestone pinned:** **ch31** the poster comes down; his public image ends; the best day of
his life; and it frightens him.

- **24.** New Year's Day, shutter down: Takafumi takes Nagi to the barbers' association shrine visit
  for the first time in her life, introduces her to no one, and on the walk back down the river path
  says three sentences, the third of which is a condition.
- **25.** The condition in full — she does not touch a head unless he is in the room, and she keeps
  a training log: a ¥120 exercise book from Bungudō Ishizuka, one line per head, date, service, and
  what she would do differently, which is the cruellest homework anybody has ever been set.
- **26.** Yaeko finds the log left on the counter, reads eleven pages of it standing up without her
  glasses, and writes corrections down the margin of the first page in red biro — forty years of
  them, in about nine minutes — and never mentions it, then or afterwards.
- **27.** The common-test weekend in mid-January: the shop is empty for four straight hours, Tōma
  spreads his work out and sits down in chair two without thinking about it, is told to get out of
  it in three words, gets out of it instantly without asking why — and asks Nagi why on the way to
  Kotori Bridge that night, and gets an answer that is about a pair of hands and not about a death.
- **28.** Early February, the slowest month of the year, two heads before noon: a routine ward
  hygiene inspection notice arrives with three weeks' warning, and Takafumi puts it under the till and
  does not mention it, and Nagi finds it on a Thursday while she is doing the float.
- **29.** The inspection: nine minutes, entirely routine, a clipboard, two questions, a signature,
  and a man who is pleasant about the weather — and Nagi is upstairs on the landing for all nine of
  them, because that was the arrangement she proposed herself, and those are the worst nine minutes
  in the series so far.
- **30.** The fourteenth of February, and the chapter declines every version of the scene you are
  braced for: she gives him nothing, he brings nothing, she cuts his hair at 19:55 in the same slot
  as the very first time — and the only person who marks the date at all is Hagiwara Kiyoshi, who brings
  a box of sweets in for Yaeko, as he has on the fourteenth for thirty years.
- **31.** **[LOCKED — THE POSTER COMES DOWN.]** Mid-March, the chain's fiscal changeover: a small
  van pulls up, a man in a company jacket unstraps the panel from the utility pole outside the
  window in under two minutes, straps up a girl from a school two stations away, and drives off —
  and Tōma watches the entire thing from chair one under a cape, and it is the best day of his life,
  and his hands will not stop shaking underneath the nylon, and Nagi keeps cutting and says nothing
  and does not stop.
- **32.** That evening he walks all four exits in one circuit to check — Kita under its cover,
  Minami behind the scratched acrylic, Nishi with its lifted corner, Higashi at eye height on the
  landing — and his face is gone from every one of them, and nobody in the ward notices for eleven
  days.
- **33.** Being nobody, in practice: he is late for something for the first time in two years and
  nothing whatsoever happens; Akie's reaction at the kitchen table is two sentences long, neither
  of them cruel and both of them wrong; and Yagisawa's reaction is four words and is the funniest
  line in the arc.
- **34.** The last day of the second year — desks stacked, lockers emptied, the room reverting to
  being a room — and Nunokawa Koharu says "Komori-san" out loud in front of everybody about something
  completely trivial, and it is the first time in the series that Nagi's name is spoken in that
  building, and it is not a triumph, it is just a name, and Nagi has to go and stand in the corridor
  for a minute.

# ARC 4 — THE WEIGHT LINE (ch35-45)

**Late March to late July, their third year. The arc where everybody decides what comes off.**

**IN-state.** No poster anywhere in the ward. A boy who has no idea what he is without one. A girl
with a training log, a condition, and a countdown at fourteen months and falling. Twenty-one
payments on the refit loan are down to one.

**OUT-state.** The countdown page is finished. The application to Keishō is posted, the money is
¥2,380,000 against ¥318,000, and the real bill is a timetable. His examination date is written on a
kitchen calendar in red. Both of them are holding a number by the end of July and neither has said
theirs out loud.

**Locked milestones pinned:** **ch38** third year begins; the university plan becomes a date on a
calendar. **ch44** she commits to the licence — training school, not university — and it costs her
something real.

- **35.** Spring holiday, the quietest week of the shop's year: the three of them repaint the
  back-room ceiling over two days with Yaeko directing from the fourth stair and never once coming
  down — and on the Friday the last payment of the 2009 refit loan leaves the account, and Takafumi
  looks at the statement for a long time and puts it in the drawer and mentions it to nobody.
- **36.** The grandmother's old fixed-blade razor comes out of the workbench drawer for the first
  time in the series — illegal on a customer, entirely legal on family — and Takafumi sits down in
  chair one, tips his head back, and lets his daughter shave his throat.
- **37.** Tsutomu and Akie Naruse come into Barber Komori as ordinary customers, knowing nothing:
  Akie books it, Tsutomu sits, and Nagi cuts a ward-office section chief's hair for ¥3,800 and hears
  him mention his son's plan twice in a completely decent voice, and says nothing at all, and does
  not correct a single hair of it.
- **38.** **[LOCKED — THIRD YEAR BEGINS.]** The second week of April: the first careers survey of
  the final year, three lines, twenty minutes — and that evening the entrance-examination date goes
  onto the Naruse kitchen calendar in the same red marker as the dentist and the gas, and stops
  being an idea.
- **39.** Golden Week: fourteen heads a day, and on the third of May Nagi does eleven of them herself
  with her father in the room the whole time — and at 20:30, with the shutter half down, he says a
  fourth sentence, which nobody in this family has ever heard him do.
- **40.** Her own careers survey comes back to her with a polite note asking her to fill it in,
  because she handed it in blank and nobody noticed for eight days — and it sits on the shop counter
  all week under the float tin, face up, where everyone can see it and nobody says anything.
- **41.** Mid-May exams: Tōma's marks slip half a grade, which is nothing, and the temperature in the
  Naruse house changes by exactly one degree, which is everything — and Yagisawa is the only person
  who notices, and says the wrong thing about it, and helps anyway.
- **42.** The rainy season: the towel cabinet loses again, the dehumidifier bucket goes to the sink
  twice a day, and the argument the two of them do not have runs eleven pages and consists entirely
  of a disagreement about what temperature the basin should be set to.
- **43.** The Keishō open day, a Sunday: forty minutes on the Tobiume Line with one transfer, alone,
  telling nobody, a practical class on a mannequin head under a real instructor who watches her for
  ninety seconds and says two sentences about her hands — and she comes home with a folder she hides
  in the towel cupboard.
- **44.** **[LOCKED — SHE COMMITS.]** Late June, fourteen months to the day from chapter 1, one
  number left on the countdown page: ¥2,380,000 against ¥318,000 in a brown envelope behind the
  ledger — and a second brown envelope in the workbench drawer, two years thick with his money, that
  she does not open. But the money is the smaller half. A day course is 09:00 to 16:00, five days a
  week, for two years: to become legal she has to take her hands out of this shop for exactly as long
  as her father's hands have left. She fills the form in at the waiting bench with the radio off and
  posts it in the box outside Bungudō Ishizuka.
- **45.** End of the first term, late July: the countdown page has nothing left on it to cross off,
  so she turns to a clean line further back in the book and writes a new number. It is a much bigger
  one. She does not tell anybody what it counts.

# ARC 5 — THE FINISH / SHIAGE (ch46-55)

**August of their third year to mid-March. Ten chapters across eight months, and it accelerates,
because that is what the last year of school actually does.**

**IN-state.** She has applied. He has a date in red on a calendar. The shop has run on four hands
for two years and one pair of them is leaving in either direction.

**OUT-state.** He goes, and it is not a tragedy. She graduates from Kōyō and starts at Keishō in
three weeks. The pole outside turns at the right speed for the first time since 2011 and nobody
will say who paid for it. And the second chair — wiped every Sunday, fed twice a year, empty for
the whole series — has somebody in it, and Nagi is standing behind it, and the shop is open.

**Locked milestones pinned:** **ch50** the leaving is decided; he goes; not a tragedy. **ch55**
finale; the second chair, occupied, Nagi behind it, the shop open.

- **46.** The fourteenth of August, the shop's one closed day of the year: eleven hours in which
  nothing whatsoever happens — the river path from Kotori Bridge to the weir and back, the ¥50 bag
  of crusts, Hanazono-yu shut for Obon so they sit on its step instead — and it is the best chapter
  in the book.
- **47.** Late September, the common-test application: the university choices written in pencil
  first and then in pen, one of them fifty minutes away and one of them four hours, and Akie has
  already written the four-hour one on the calendar in a hand that is not unkind.
- **48.** The second weekend of October, their last culture festival: this time they come through
  the gate together in front of everybody, and the entire consequence is that Momose Rurika screams
  once, Yagisawa says five words, and Nunokawa Koharu does not look up from the float tin.
- **49.** November, Yaeko's hands, honestly: the leather-food tin she cannot open, the jar lid, the
  eleven-second fish that now takes forty — and a conversation on the fourth stair about chair two
  that is not a death sentence and is, in its own way, worse.
- **50.** **[LOCKED — THE LEAVING IS DECIDED.]** December: he chooses the four-hour one, at a
  kitchen table, in about ninety seconds, and it is his decision and not his mother's — and Nagi is
  the person who makes it possible, with one sentence, and the sentence is an instruction about a
  towel, and it is the only thing she says all evening.
- **51.** The last pre-New-Year rush she will ever work as a schoolgirl: the thirtieth of December,
  queue on the pavement, fourteen hours — and Takafumi calls her name for the next head, out loud, in
  front of forty people, which is either nothing at all or the largest thing that happens in arc 5.
- **52.** Mid-January, the morning of the common test: she cuts his hair at 06:30 in a shop that is
  not open, one bank of lights on, no till, no book, no money — and walks him to Higashi-guchi and
  does not go through the gate.
- **53.** Late February, the slowest fortnight of the shop's year and the whole of his exam period:
  two heads before noon, the accounts spread out on the waiting bench with the radio off, and a thin
  envelope on the mat with his name on it that gets opened in the shop, because the shop is where he
  opens things now.
- **54.** Early March, the moving: what fits in a small van, what he leaves in the back room without
  mentioning it — and on the last morning the striped pole outside is turning at the right speed for
  the first time since 2011, with nobody admitting who called the man, and a brown envelope in the
  workbench drawer that is empty.
- **55.** **[LOCKED — FINALE.]** Mid-March: the graduation ceremony in the morning, and Barber
  Komori open in the afternoon with the shutter up and the bell going twice — **Yaeko in the second
  chair**, for the first time in the series and the first time in her life as a customer, with a
  towel round her shoulders and her eyes shut; **Nagi standing behind it** with a comb and her
  grandmother's shears; Takafumi at chair one with Tōma in it, home for four days, under a cape. And
  the last line in the manga is **"HOW SHORT?"** — asked at chair one, by somebody else. **The shop
  is open.**
