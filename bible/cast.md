## 1. WORLD RENDER — BASELINE

Render-relevant only. If it cannot be drawn, it is not in this section.

### KŌYŌ HIGH SCHOOL UNIFORM — locked

**Blazer, not gakuran.** Single-breasted, notch lapel, **one button fastened**, no vents drawn.

| Garment | Value |
|---|---|
| Blazer, trousers, skirt | **30% dot screentone**, flat, no gradient. Never solid black. |
| Shirt / blouse | **pure white paper**, zero tone |
| Boys' necktie / girls' ribbon | **flat solid black** — the only black accent above the waist |
| Winter knit vest | 20% tone, worn under the blazer |
| Indoor shoes | white, with a **toe band**: **solid black = second year, untoned white = first year** |

- Skirt is a plain box pleat, tone with fine white pleat lines. Trousers straight, no cuff.
- **No crest, no badge, no lettering anywhere on the uniform** (signage law). The school's one
  identifier is a **fine white piping line along the outer edge of each blazer cuff** — draw it,
  it is the whole insignia.
- **Summer:** blazer off, white short-sleeve shirt, tie/ribbon still worn. Tōma keeps his tie
  knotted in August. Nagi's ribbon is simply absent June to September and nobody mentions it.

**How the two leads wear it — the difference is the characterisation:**

- **TŌMA — immaculate.** Blazer buttoned, top shirt button done, tie knot tight against the
  collar and **drawn at the same size in every panel** (use a template; the invariance is the
  point). Cuffs at the wrist bone. Bag carried in the hand, never slung. He is not smart, he is
  *correct*, and the correctness is the exhaustion made visible.
- **NAGI — utilitarian.** Blazer worn open, always. Ribbon tied but sitting a half-turn
  off-centre. Shirt cuffs **buttoned at school** (the one rule she keeps — see her state-switch).
  Single bag strap hooked on the elbow, never both shoulders: **her hands stay free.** Scuffed
  white on the toe of her shoes. Nothing is sloppy; everything is stripped for speed.

### NAGI'S SHOP WEAR — locked

**Apron, not a smock.** A **barber's straight-cut bib apron in FLAT SOLID BLACK**, ties crossed
at the back and knotted in front, worn over her school blouse — she does not change out of her
uniform, she changes *into* the apron. Effect, and this is the entire point: **at school she is
a tone-grey figure; in the shop she becomes a solid black torso from chest to knee.** Two
shapes, one girl, zero ambiguity. Against Tōma's huge white cape, shop pages read as graphics.

- Sleeves rolled **twice, above the elbow**. Forearms bare, drawn with a tendon line.
- **Scissor sheath on the left hip**: three pairs, handles up. One pair has **white tape wound
  round the finger ring** — that pair is hers, it was Yae's, and it is a signature prop.
- Flat black lace-ups kept under the counter and changed at the door. The shoe change is the
  threshold: last panel of school, first panel of shop.
- A **wide-tooth comb pushed through her hair**, and **two steel bobby pins** held in her teeth
  while she re-ties it.

**THE STATE-SWITCH — Nagi's whole visual grammar.** The single most important line in this file:

> **At school her hair is pulled up so hard that her own crown whorl is exposed. In the shop it
> is re-tied high and loose and the whorl is covered.**
> **Whorl drawn = School Nagi. Whorl hidden = Shop Nagi.** No exceptions, every panel.

School: hair scraped up, twisted into a **flat coil pinned at the nape with two steel pins
crossed in an X**; the hairline shows; the silhouette is small, closed, unremarkable — and the
tsumuji sits on top of her head all day where she cannot see it and nobody looks. In the shop:
pins out (into the teeth), re-tied **high and loose with the comb driven through it**, volume
over the crown, **one long loose strand falling on her left** that she blows off her face.
Second, redundant tell for the artist: **cuffs buttoned at school, rolled twice in the shop.**
When school-Nagi is genuinely engaged, **one sleeve creeps up without her noticing** — that is
the leak, and it is worth a panel.

### TŌMA'S OUT-OF-SCHOOL WEAR — locked

Plain crew-neck knit (**pure white, no tone**) over a shirt, straight dark trousers (30% tone),
plain low sneakers, a flat-crown cotton cap in winter, a grey scarf. Every item correct, every
item bought by his mother, no item chosen. **Nothing he owns carries lettering, ever** — the boy
whose face is printed at three times life size does not own a printed word.

The clothes are a costume of neutrality. The arc agent may spend the **one** item he buys for
himself (proposal: a second-hand grey work jacket with a broken zip from the shopping street's
dry-goods shop, around ch33 — an object, not a speech).

### BARBER KOMORI — RENDERING CANON

Geography, fixed for all chapters: **street window and door front-left; two chairs facing the
long mirror wall; basin back-left; hot-towel cabinet on the back wall; till on a counter beside
the door; three-seat waiting bench under the window; stairs up at the back-right.** Chair one is
the street-side chair. **Chair two — Yae's — is the wall-side chair.**

- **THE MIRROR WALL.** Drawn as **pure white paper with one fine black line for the frame edge.**
  It holds a reflection **only when the script says so**; otherwise, white. A reflection contains
  **faces and shoulders only, cropped, on white — never a reflected room** (a reflected interior
  reads as painting and is banned). Reflections are drawn **cleaner than the direct shot**: fewer
  lines, no tone on clothing.
- **THE CHAIR (chrome).** Outline only, plus **two or three short white sickle-shaped gaps left
  in the black line** to read as chrome. Never grey gradient. Leather back and seat in **flat
  solid black**; chair one carries **two fine crack lines on the front seat edge**, chair two
  carries none and never will.
- **THE CAPE.** A **LARGE PURE-WHITE SHAPE, zero tone** — the biggest white mass on any shop
  page, nape to footrest, described with **three to five long fold lines and nothing else.**
  Never crumpled, never shaded. The cape is the page's lung. Off the shoulders it is **snapped**
  (SFX BASA) and the flecks go up.
- **HAIR ON THE FLOOR.** **Fine scattered ink flecks** — short tapered strokes 1-3mm, densest in
  a rough arc under the chair, thinning outward; a few single hairs stuck to the cape and to her
  bare forearm. **Never a pile, never a tone blob.** The floor is a clock inside the chapter:
  clean at the start, drifted mid-scene, swept by the end.
- **THE POLE.** A cylinder in repeating diagonal bands: **black / white / 30% tone**, in that
  order. **One hairline crack runs from the top cap down through two bands, in the same place
  every time.** It has turned at the wrong speed since 2011. Drawn from outside through the
  window, or as a slice at a panel edge — never centre-frame, never with motion lines more than
  once a chapter. Its SFX carries a stutter (UIIN... UI-IN...) until the chapter somebody fixes
  it, and runs steady (UIIN) forever after.
- **The wall of framed licences:** three frames, contents **illegible** — flat white rectangles
  with a fine frame line and one diagonal white glare bar (signage law).
- **The appointment book:** a soft ruled notebook, always drawn **open on the current spread**,
  entries in pencil, her handwriting small and upright.
- **The till:** a mechanical drawer register, black box with a white face. **It sticks.** Two
  sounds — **GACHAN** when it closes and **GAKON** when it does not — and it needs a hip-bump.
- **The hot-towel cabinet:** a small chrome box, white-gap highlights, its door drawn open only
  at the instant a towel is taken.

### THE POSTER OF TŌMA — RENDERING CANON

Four station exits. The same photograph since he was fifteen: head and shoulders, three times
life size, a slight closed-mouth smile.

- **PRINT LAW.** The poster's face is rendered in a **visibly coarser dot screen than anything
  else on the page** — bigger dots, wider spacing. It must read as *printed matter inside a
  drawn world*. This one rule does more work than any dialogue could.
- **NEVER CLEAN, NEVER WHOLE.** Every appearance is cropped by the panel edge, angled off-axis,
  or interrupted — a passing head, a bicycle, the pole, a rain streak, the lifting corner.
  **Minimum one occluder or crop, always.** The reader may never once see that face square and
  entire. The series' thesis, expressed as a framing rule.
- **NO LEGIBLE TEXT.** Any wording is cropped away, turned, or covered by a glare bar. The
  poster says nothing. It only looks.
- **THE WEATHERING LADDER (locked, one state per band):**

| Ch | State |
|---|---|
| 1-4 | Flat, near-new. One small air bubble under the surface at the jaw. |
| 5-9 | Top-left corner lifted a few millimetres. |
| 10-14 | Corner curled a finger's width. A rain-run streak from the eye downward. **No character ever remarks on where that streak falls.** |
| 15-19 | Sun-bleached: the poster's dot screen goes finer and lighter. The face pales. |
| 20-23 | Somebody has drawn on it — small, harmless, **on the shoulder, never the face**: a stick figure with a cat's face. He likes it. |
| 24-27 | A smaller new poster pasted over its bottom edge, covering the collar. |
| 28-30 | A tear across the top corner; the curl is now a flap that moves. |
| **31** | Removed. |
| 32-55 | **The ghost:** a rectangle of unfaded board, four staple holes, one triangle of paper left in a corner. It appears occasionally and **carries no dialogue about it, ever.** |

### THE ELDERS ARE NOT SMALL

House law, because generators get this wrong: **Yae (74) and Hagiwara (79) are drawn tall,
long-boned and thinned by age — 7.5 heads, long neck, narrow dropped shoulders, long fingers.**
Age is drawn with **line quality**: fine lines at the eye and mouth corners, thinner hair, veins
and knuckles on the hands, a slight forward carry of the head. **Never** with shrunken, hunched,
squat or blocky proportion. Yae is the tallest woman in the series.

## 2. THE LEADS

# KOMORI NAGI — 小森 凪, 17, second year

Third generation at BARBER KOMORI, three stations from school. She has held shears since she
was eight, when her grandmother put a pair in her hand and never took them back.
She is objectively better than her father and everyone in the shop knows it, including him.
Seventeen, unlicensed, fourteen months from being legally allowed to touch a stranger's head —
which has not stopped her, and she has done the arithmetic on exactly what that costs.

### SCHOOL FACE

Back row by the window, out of the door within ninety seconds of the bell. Nothing about her is
a performance of sadness — she is not bullied, not lonely in a way she would recognise, not
building to a breakdown. She is **economical**. School is an expense she is minimising. She
answers the smallest true part of any question and attaches a departure to it, and she is very
good at not being asked a second one; three girls would call her nice and none of them could
name one thing she likes. She sits by the window because of the draught, which is true, and
because the window faces the direction of the shop, which she has never said to anybody. The
only thing at school that is entirely hers: she knows every classmate's hairline by heart —
whose crown is double, whose fringe is fighting a cowlick, who has been cutting their own
fringe in a bathroom — and in two and a half years she has never once mentioned it.

### TRUE FACE

The shoe change at the shop door is the switch, and everything downstream of it is a different
animal: fast, certain, loud, technical, filthy with the regulars in the specific way that only
happens between people who have known each other since one of them was small. She has no
audience anxiety because the shop was never an audience. She takes the piss out of a
seventy-nine-year-old and hands him his coat with both hands. She is the most competent person
in any room she stands in, and the shop is the only room where that is visible to anyone. And
under it, unspoken and precisely understood: every head she touches puts her father's licence,
her father's shop and her own future on the counter. She has priced it. She keeps cutting.

### VOICE

**At school:** short, flat, deflecting; two bubbles maximum in a row. She asks no questions,
uses no adjectives, offers no follow-up, and ends on an exit. **In the shop:** fast, blunt,
warm, technical, funny by being literal and unimpressed. Her grammar is **instructions and
measurements** — she speaks in millimetres, minutes, angles and prices, and a huge proportion of
her lines are imperatives aimed at a head. She never softens a sentence; she softens by doing
something gentle with her hands while she says the hard version. She almost never uses a
person's name — she addresses the head ("chin", "down", "other side"), which is why the first
time she uses Tōma's name it detonates. Her one ceremonial line, said to every customer who has
ever sat down, is **"How short?"**

**SAMPLE LINES — public (school):**
- "Window seat. It's the draught."
- "I've got somewhere at four."
- "Take the notes. I won't need them."
- "Sorry, I'm bad at group ones."
- "Ask Nunokawa. She was actually listening."
- "It's not mine. Someone left it."
- "You can have the milk. Honestly."
- "Fine. Yeah. See you Monday."

**SAMPLE LINES — private (shop):**
- "Chin to your chest. Further. Good."
- "Don't nod. Nod and you're bald."
- "Your whorl fights me. It always did."
- "That's not a cowlick. That's weather."
- "Hold still. This is the illegal bit."
- "You want it neat, or yours?"
- "Sit down, you awful old man."
- "Dad. The till. Hip it."

*Signature line, exempt from the four-word floor (see `style.md` §0): **"How short?"** — her
first line to every customer who has ever sat down, including him, forever.*

### ARTIST LOCK — KOMORI NAGI

KOMORI NAGI, 17. 168cm, drawn at 7.75 heads — small head, long slim neck, narrow gently sloping
shoulders, long forearms, legs slightly more than half of total height, flat tapering torso.
Never compact, never square-shouldered, never low-centred. Her hands are the most carefully
drawn feature in the series: long fingers, visible knuckles and tendon lines, short unpolished
nails. HAIR: flat solid black, blunt and heavy, always worn up — she is the black shape in every
two-shot. SCHOOL: scraped into a flat coil, two steel pins crossed in an X at the nape, hairline

open, ribbon a half-turn off-centre, cuffs buttoned at school; in the shop a flat solid-black
bib apron, sleeves rolled twice. PROPS: the taped-ring shears at her left hip, two bobby pins in
her teeth. TRUE-FACE TELL: whorl covered and sleeves rolled.

# NARUSE TŌMA — 成瀬 冬馬, 17, second year, different class

Topped the prefectural mock exams at fifteen; a cram-school chain bought the use of his face and
has had it at four station exits ever since, three times life size, the same photograph in every
season. Polite, dry, unarrogant, genuinely clever, genuinely kind, and tired in a way that has
no incident behind it and therefore no cure. He has no idea what he wants, which at seventeen is
a far lonelier problem than it sounds, and he has never once said it out loud.

### SCHOOL FACE

He is not popular. He is **known**, which is different and worse. Teachers relax when they see
him at the front of a corridor. Classmates hand him things to hold. Younger students take
photographs of the board over his shoulder as if his angle on it were better. He has a working
script for every interaction in the building and it has never once failed him, which is exactly
the problem: nothing has ever gone wrong badly enough to require a real reaction. He is
unfailingly generous with the only currency he has — he will explain anything to anyone for as
long as they want it, and he will not let them feel stupid, and he means it. The tell that
something is missing is small and constant, and it takes the reader most of a chapter to name
it: **he never asks anybody anything.** Two years of being the person who is checked, and he has
lost the habit of wondering out loud.

### TRUE FACE

In a chair, facing a mirror, with a cape over his hands and somebody's fingers in his hair, he
finds a register with no script in it — and what comes out is **questions**. Small ones, sincere
ones, slightly strange ones, at a rate that suggests two years of backlog: how do you know, what
does it look like, does everyone's do that, what happens after you close. He is dry in a low
deadpan that startles her the first time and delights her the second. He is honest the way
people are honest when nothing they say is going to be graded. And he is, quietly, the only
person in the series who finds Nagi funny **on purpose** — he sets her up, waits, and never once
lets on that he did it.

### VOICE

**In public:** immaculate, complete, empty. **Mechanical rule — no contractions and no
questions.** "That is kind." "I do not mind waiting." The formality reads as manners; it is
actually the bars. **In the chair:** contractions return and the questions start. **Rule: at
least one genuine question from him per shop scene**, and through the early chapters he asks
more than she answers. His humour is arrived at flatly and never signposted — no wordplay, no
punchline face; he simply says the true thing in the driest possible order and lets it sit. He
states facts about himself as though reading them off a list, with no self-pity at all, which is
precisely why they land. When he is frightened, the public register comes back — full sentences,
no contractions — and the reader learns to hear that as an alarm.

**SAMPLE LINES — public (school, street, home):**
- "That is kind. Thank you."
- "I will have it back Friday."
- "No, please. You go ahead."
- "It was a fair question, honestly."
- "I do not mind waiting. Really."
- "Congratulations. You worked for that."
- "My mother sends her thanks."
- "Anytime. I mean that."

**SAMPLE LINES — private (the chair):**
- "How do you know when to stop?"
- "What does the back look like?"
- "Does everyone's hair do that? Or mine?"
- "The salon never asked me anything."
- "Is that the pole? It sounds ill."
- "Can I sit until you sweep?"
- "Your father says three things. Exactly three."
- "What do you do after closing?"

### ARTIST LOCK — NARUSE TŌMA

NARUSE TŌMA, 17. 176cm, drawn at 8 heads — small head, long neck, narrow sloping shoulders that
he holds level and still, long arms, long legs, slim flat torso. Never broad, never blocky,
never square. HAIR: light — 20% dot screentone with a wide clean white shine band — against
Nagi's solid black, so any two-shot separates instantly. His crown whorl sits left of centre and
lifts; it is the reason his hair has never sat right. NAPE LAW, the series' clock: square-cut
nape = the chain salon and the poster; soft pointed nape, needle sideburns, fringe broken into
fine points = Nagi's cut. Any panel can be dated from the back of his head. EYES: long straight
upper lashes, heavy lash line meeting the iris, irises in fine vertical striations, EXACTLY ONE
small square highlight, always — except in mirror reflections, where a second tiny highlight
appears. The glass sees what the room does not. CLOTHES: blazer buttoned, top button done, tie
knot identical in every panel; in the chair the top button opens and the knot drops exactly one
finger-width. PROPS: a mechanical pencil worn shiny, clip snapped off; a furred, over-folded
timetable card. TRUE-FACE TELL: tie loosened one finger-width, and a small tented movement under
the white cape — his hands are not calm.

### TWO-SHOT LAW

In any panel containing both leads: **she is the black mass, he is the white/light mass.** In the
shop this is absolute — solid black apron against pure white cape, solid black hair against 20%
tone. At school the contrast inverts and goes quiet: both are 30%-tone blazers, both are grey,
and that flatness is the point. **The shop is the only place either of them has a silhouette.**

## 3. SUPPORTING CAST

### KOMORI TAKAFUMI — 小森 孝文, 48 · Nagi's father · second-generation barber

Extraordinary with hair, hopeless with sentences. The shopping street has called him **Nidaime**
— the Second — for twenty years and he has never decided whether he minds. He raised Nagi in the
shop because there was nowhere else to put her. (Her mother lives two prefectures away,
remarried, on good terms; they text about the weather. It is not a wound and it is never a plot
point.) He knows his daughter is better than he is. He has known it for four years. He has not
found the three words.

**Public/private:** he says the same three beats everywhere — the difference is that in public
all three are instructions, and in private the third one is a feeling wearing an instruction's
clothes.

**SAMPLE LINES:**
- "Wet it. Comb it. Look."
- "Too fast. Too proud. Again."
- "Sit. Eat. Then talk."
- "Not the pole. Not yet. No."
- "Good hands. Bad temper. Mine too."
- "Boy again. Third time. Fine."
- "Lock up. Lights. Bed."

**ARTIST LOCK — KOMORI TAKAFUMI.** 48. 178cm, drawn at 7.75 heads — long-boned and narrow,
sloping shoulders, long forearms, a slight forward set to the neck from thirty years of standing
over a chair. Never bulky, never squat, never square. HAIR: 20% tone, white at the temples only
(leave the temples as white paper), clipped so short at the sides it reads as texture rather
than shape; his own crown is thinning and he cuts around it exactly the way Nagi cuts around
Tōma's — same hand, one generation up. EYES: heavy straight lash line, small irises, ONE
highlight, lids sitting low; he always looks like a man about to say something who then does
not. CLOTHES: a white barber's tunic buttoned at the shoulder (pure white paper, zero tone),
sleeves to the elbow, a comb in the breast pocket in every single panel. PROP: a straight razor
he strops every morning and no longer uses on strangers.

### KOMORI YAEKO — 小森 八重子, 74 · "Yae" · retired barber, lives upstairs

Opened the shop in 1968 and has outlasted almost everything in it, including the pole's first
motor. She retired at seventy-one for arthritis and boredom, in that order, and she will tell
you the boredom was considerably worse. She taught Nagi everything, including which things not
to say, and she is **the only person alive who has seen both of Nagi's faces** — a fact she has
never once mentioned to either of the people it concerns. **She is alive at chapter 55.**

**Public/private:** downstairs she is the shop's charming retired queen. Upstairs, or at a
chair, she is a foreman — and the switch is audible: she says "Nagi-chan" until she is speaking
to her granddaughter **as a barber**, and then she says "**Komori**," flat, like a surname on a
roster. Everyone in the room straightens up.

**SAMPLE LINES:**
- "Your grandfather cut like a butcher."
- "Sit down. You're blocking my programme."
- "That boy's coming back. Obviously."
- "I cut my own fringe. Deal."
- "Komori. Your left hand is lying."
- "Forty years. Nobody thanked the chair."
- "Don't tell your father. Not yet."

**ARTIST LOCK — KOMORI YAEKO.** 74. 170cm and STILL TALL — 7.5 heads, long spine, long neck,
narrow shoulders dropped rather than hunched, long thin hands with prominent knuckles and two
crooked finger joints. NEVER draw her small, round, bent or squat: she is the tallest woman in
the series and she stands like somebody who spent fifty years not sitting down. Age is line
quality only — fine creases at the eye and mouth corners, thin skin over the hands. HAIR: 10%
tone (near white) with fine strand lines, a short crop she cuts herself, and a fringe that is
visibly, deliberately uneven. EYES: single fine lash line, small dark irises, ONE highlight, and
the outer corners lift — she appears amused in every panel, including the ones where she is not.
CLOTHES: cardigan over a plain blouse, 30% tone, **sleeves pushed up exactly like her
granddaughter's.** PROP: a comb she carries everywhere and never uses.

### NUNOKAWA KOHARU — 17 · class 2-B · **the classmate who sees (ch12)**

The girl who runs the handouts and takes the notes nobody asked her to take. Two years into
braces, kind in an unglamorous way that has no strategy anywhere in it. **Her orthodontist is on
the shopping street** and her appointment is every third Thursday at six, which is why she is
standing outside a barbershop at twenty to seven in chapter 12 holding somebody else's homework.
She is the one who sees. It matters because she is the last person in the school who would ever
tell — and because she is the only one who had already noticed Komori Nagi existed.

**Public/private:** in public she over-apologises and over-explains. In private she is
startlingly direct — one flat sentence, no cushion — and then covers her mouth with her hand.

**SAMPLE LINES:**
- "You dropped this. Two streets back."
- "I won't say. I'm not like that."
- "My orthodontist's on that street. Sorry."
- "You looked different. That's all I meant."
- "Can I just sit here? Quietly?"
- "I brought two. One's yours."
- "I'm bad at pretending I didn't."

**ARTIST LOCK — NUNOKAWA KOHARU.** 17. 163cm, 7.5 heads, narrow sloping shoulders, long thin
arms usually folded across a stack of handouts, long legs, slight frame. Not small — slim, and
slightly too tall for how quietly she moves. Never stocky, never rounded. HAIR: **30% dot tone**
— a deliberate mid value between the two leads so she never competes for the eye in a three-shot
— shoulder length, held off one side with a plain clip, soft flat fringe. EYES: the roundest in
the cast; large, wide open, full lower lid, TWO highlights (a large upper and a small lower),
which reads instantly as harmless and is accurate. BRACES: one fine horizontal line across the
upper teeth, and she covers her mouth with the back of her hand when she laughs. CLOTHES:
uniform exactly to code, blazer buttoned, **both bag straps on both shoulders** — the mechanical
opposite of Nagi in every panel they share.

### YAGISAWA GAKU — 17 · broadcasting club · **Tōma's one real friend**

Reads the lunchtime announcements badly, on purpose, and has been quietly warned about it twice.
He has known Tōma since the entrance ceremony and has **never once** asked him about a score, a
rank, a university, or the poster. Instead he asks him things that do not matter — relentlessly,
daily, with total commitment — and Tōma answers them with complete seriousness, because they are
the only questions anybody puts to him that he is allowed to get wrong. That is the whole
friendship, and it is load-bearing, and neither of them will ever describe it.

**Public/private:** one register everywhere. The only difference with Tōma is that he lets the
silences run instead of filling them.

**SAMPLE LINES:**
- "Second-floor machine. The cold one. Discuss."
- "You're doing the polite face again."
- "I don't want your notes, Naruse."
- "New haircut. Somebody's touching your head."
- "Wrong. It's obviously the ramen one."
- "I'll read the lunch news badly."
- "Sit with us. Or don't. Whatever."

**ARTIST LOCK — YAGISAWA GAKU.** 17. 172cm, 7.75 heads, narrow sloping shoulders, long loose
limbs, and the worst posture in the series — draw the full length first, THEN let it slouch;
never shorten him to make him slouch. HAIR: flat solid black like Nagi's, but **short and
spike-cropped with a blunt straight fringe**, so silhouette alone separates them at any
distance. EYES: small and narrow, single flat lash line, ONE highlight, and one eyebrow drawn
permanently a fraction higher than the other. CLOTHES: uniform worn wrong in cheerful, specific
ways — tie knotted long and loose, blazer sleeves shoved up the forearm, top two buttons open,
bag hanging off one finger. PROP: a handheld microphone he is not authorised to be carrying
around the building.

### HAGIWARA KIYOSHI — 79 · retired watch repairer · **the oldest regular**

Chair one, every third Saturday, forty-one years. He repaired watches on the shopping street
until his hands went, and he can still name the fault in any mechanism by sound alone. He calls
Nagi by her grandmother's name about half the time, and **the mechanic is exact: "Yae-chan" when
he is looking at her in the mirror, "Nagi-chan" when he turns round and looks at her directly.**
In the glass, at that angle, over a white cape, she is Yae at twenty. Takafumi corrected him
once, early. Yae told him to stop correcting him.

**Payoff, locked:** he is the one who eventually fixes the pole. Shaking hands, no speech.

**SAMPLE LINES:**
- "Yae-chan. Not too much off top."
- "Forty-one years. Same chair. Same girl."
- "That pole's a quartz problem. Solvable."
- "Don't rush. Springs remember being rushed."
- "Nagi-chan. Sorry. I did it again."
- "Your hands are steadier than mine."
- "Third Saturday. I'll be here."

**ARTIST LOCK — HAGIWARA KIYOSHI.** 79. 174cm and long — 7.5 heads, long shins, long forearms,
narrow shoulders dropped low, a slight forward carry of the head. He is **thin, not shrunken**:
never squat, never round, never bent double. HAIR: 10% tone (near white), fine strands, thin on
top with the scalp left as white paper — his own crown is bare and the series draws it without
ever commenting. EYES: deep lids, fine lash line, small irises, ONE highlight, heavy lower-lid
lines. His HANDS are the point: long, spotted, drawn with visible tendon and knuckle, and they
tremble — indicate the tremor with **a second fainter contour line beside the fingers**, never
with motion arcs. CLOTHES: a jacket over a buttoned shirt, 30% tone, always neat, always very
slightly too large for him now.

### MOMOSE RURIKA — 16, first year · art club · **the girl in love with the poster**

That photograph has been her phone wallpaper since she was fourteen, and she has drawn it, by
her own count, ninety-odd times. She has been in the same building as Naruse Tōma for four
months and has not recognised him once — because the poster is three times life size and he is
not, and because she has never looked at the real one for longer than a second. **She is not a
rival and never becomes one.** What she loses is an image; what she grieves is an image; what
she keeps is a friend and a firm opinion that Tōma is entitled to be nobody. She is also the
first person at school to notice that the back of his head changed, because she is the only
person at school who has ever studied it.

**Payoff, locked:** when the poster comes down at 31, **she takes it home.**

**Public/private:** in public, bright, fast, half a volume too loud. In private, precise — an
artist's precision, aimed at faces, and it is unnerving.

**SAMPLE LINES:**
- "Senpai, your nape changed. In March."
- "I've drawn that face ninety times."
- "He's shorter in person. Much shorter."
- "It isn't the same thing. Obviously."
- "Can I have it? When it comes down?"
- "Nagi-senpai, you're scary. I like it."
- "I liked a picture. That's allowed."

**ARTIST LOCK — MOMOSE RURIKA.** 16, first year. 160cm — the shortest in the cast and STILL 7.5
heads: small head, long slim neck, narrow sloping shoulders, long thin arms, long legs. Short is
achieved by **scale only, never by thickening**, and she is never chibi-proportioned outside a
[CHIBI] panel. HAIR: 20% tone like Tōma's, but LONG, in two low tails tied with plain elastics,
with a heavy blunt fringe cut straight across just above the eyebrows. EYES: very large and
round, TWO highlights plus a small third catch low in the iris — the most rendered eyes in the
series, because she looks harder at faces than anybody in it. CLOTHES: uniform to code, indoor
shoes with an **untoned white toe band** — that band is how the page states her year without a
word of dialogue. PROPS: a small hardback sketchbook, always shut; a graphite-blackened right
little finger.

### NARUSE AKIE — 成瀬 明恵, 49 · Tōma's mother · dispensing pharmacist

She runs the dispensary attached to a clinic two stations up the line, where her professional
life is correct amounts, delivered on time, to people who are worse off than they admit. She
loves her son with exactly that instrument. She books his haircuts, his dentist, his mocks and
his winter coat, and every one of those bookings is an act of love that nobody in the house has
ever managed to call by any other name. She is not a villain and she is never wrong about a fact.

**Public/private:** identical, and that is the trouble. She speaks to her son in the register
she uses at the counter — clear, warm, complete, and unarguable.

**SAMPLE LINES:**
- "Two more years. Then you rest."
- "I booked your haircut for Sunday."
- "You've lost weight. I weighed you."
- "It's not pressure. It's a schedule."
- "Eat first. The rest waits."
- "I know exactly what he needs."
- "Your father cried. He denies it."

**ARTIST LOCK — NARUSE AKIE.** 49. 167cm, 7.5 heads, narrow sloping shoulders, long neck, long
slim hands usually still and folded. Upright and unhurried; never stocky, never square. HAIR:
**20% tone, matching Tōma's exactly** — the family resemblance lives in the VALUE, and it is how
the reader knows whose house this is — short bob, tucked behind one ear, not a strand out of
place in fifty-five chapters. EYES: Tōma's shape precisely (long straight upper lashes, ONE
small square highlight), but with the lower lid raised in every panel, so she always appears to
be almost smiling and never quite is. CLOTHES: plain blouse and cardigan at home, both untoned
white; at work a white coat drawn as pure paper. PROP: a phone held flat in her open palm with
the calendar showing.

### NARUSE TSUTOMU — 成瀬 努, 51 · Tōma's father · checking draughtsman

For twenty-six years he has checked other people's structural drawings for the one wrong figure.
He was not clever and he says so without bitterness, as a measurement. He believes effort is a
form of love because it was the only form available to him, and he has given his son every hour
of it. **He is not a villain.** He is a man who found the one wrong number in his own life and
has never been able to stop looking for it in everybody else's.

**Public/private:** in public he defers to his wife on every subject including his son. Alone
with Tōma he is shy, plainer, and accidentally devastating.

**SAMPLE LINES:**
- "Show me the whole sheet, Tōma."
- "One wrong figure. Everything else collapses."
- "I was not clever. You are."
- "The ward is small. That's all."
- "Do not thank me. Just go."
- "I checked. Twice. It's correct."
- "Your mother is usually right."

**ARTIST LOCK — NARUSE TSUTOMU.** 51. 175cm, 7.5 heads, long-boned, narrow sloping shoulders,
long forearms, a desk-worker's forward-set neck. Thin rather than heavy; never blocky, never
square, never low-centred. HAIR: 30% tone, receded at the temples with the corners left as white
paper, combed flat and parted low — **the exact haircut his son wears on the poster**, which the
series never once says out loud. EYES: small, deep-set, single fine lash line, ONE highlight,
lower lids drawn straight so his face reads as attention without warmth even when the warmth is
real. CLOTHES: shirt buttoned to the collar with no tie at home, trousers 30% tone, house
slippers. PROPS: reading glasses pushed up onto his forehead and forgotten; a red pencil.

### THE BENCH (named so writers stop inventing)

Three more regulars exist and may fill chair one whenever a chapter needs a body: **Ōzeki**, who

## 4. RELATIONSHIP MAP

### WHO KNOWS WHAT AT SERIES START

**Nobody knows anything. The reader learns both true faces in chapter 1, and is the only one
holding both halves until chapter 12.**

- **Nobody at school** knows Nagi cuts hair, that a shop exists, or that she is good at
  anything. Three girls know her name. Not one could name a thing she likes.
- **Nobody at the shop** knows Nagi is invisible at school. The regulars have only ever met the
  loud one and assume that is simply who she is everywhere. It has never come up.
- **Takafumi** knows she "tidies up family." He does **not** know she has been cutting
  strangers. That gap is ch23 and he is not angry when it closes.
- **Yae** is the only living person who has seen both faces — and she has never said so, to
  either of the two people it concerns, in three years.
- **Tōma's parents** do not know he gets his hair cut anywhere but the chain salon; his mother
  books it. The first crack in the whole edifice is a cancelled Sunday appointment.
- **Gaku** knows nothing and suspects everything, and expresses the suspicion only by asking
  stupider questions.
- **Rurika** knows the face and not the person, and does not know they are the same species.
- **Koharu** knows Nagi as "the girl who leaves fast" — she is the only one at school who has
  bothered to notice her at all, which is precisely why she is the one who sees at 12.
- **Hagiwara** knows Nagi as Yae's granddaughter, and half the time as Yae.
- **Neither lead knows the other exists** before page 1. Same year, different class, two years
  in the same building, never once spoken. That is not a contrivance; that is a school.
