# TSUMUJI — SHOWRUNNER PILLARS (locked)

つむじ / TSUMUJI — *the crown whorl; the one part of yourself you will never see*

This file is the constitution. Bible files (series.md, cast.md, style.md, arc.md) expand it;
nothing in them may contradict it.

**GENRE, LOCKED:** modern high-school slice-of-life ROMANCE, warm and quiet. Two people whose
school selves and private selves are exact inversions of each other. The comedy is *texture* —
gentle, dry, constant — and never the engine. No gag premise, no comedy club, no performance.
The engine is intimacy: being seen by one person in the place you cannot see yourself.

**STRICTLY 55 CHAPTERS. One complete story. Chapter 55 is the finale and it lands.**

STANDALONE LAW: shares nothing with any other property. No shared universe, names, places,
props, gags, or motifs.

---

## LOGLINE

Everyone in the ward knows Naruse Tōma's face, because it has been on the cram-school posters
at four station exits, at three times life size, since he was fifteen. Nobody has looked at
him — the person — in two years. Komori Nagi is invisible at school on purpose and sovereign
three stations away, in her family's two-chair barbershop, where she has been holding scissors
since she was eight and is better than her father and cannot legally touch a customer's head
for another fourteen months. He walks in five minutes before closing. His own face is on the
pole outside the window. She doesn't look at it. She asks how short.

**TAGLINE:** There's one part of you that you will never see. She sees it every three weeks.

## THE TWO LEADS (locked)

**KOMORI NAGI (小森 凪), 17, second year.**
At school: deliberately unremarkable. Hair scraped up off her neck, plain frames, back row by
the window, out the door within ninety seconds of the bell. She is not bullied and she is not
sad — she is *economical*. School is a place she is waiting out. She has three acquaintances
and no enemies and nobody could tell you one thing about her.
In the shop: a completely different animal — fast, certain, funny, filthy-mouthed with the
regulars, hands that have known what they're doing since she was eight. Third generation at
BARBER KOMORI. She is, objectively, better than her father, and everyone in the shop knows it
including him.
**Her structural problem:** she is seventeen and **unlicensed**. In this country you cannot
legally cut a customer's hair without a barber's licence, and you cannot sit the exam until
you have done the training. Every head she touches is illegal, and if it ever comes out it is
her father's shop, his licence, and her own future that go. She has been "just tidying up
family" for three years. Tōma is the first stranger.

**NARUSE TŌMA (成瀬 冬馬), 17, same year, different class.**
Topped the prefectural mock exams at fifteen; a cram-school chain bought the right to use his
face; for two years he has been on posters at four station exits — same photo, same slight
smile, weathering season by season. Everyone in the ward has seen him at 3x life size. He is
polite, dry, quietly funny, unarrogant, and *tired*. Not tortured, not damaged — just a person
who has not been in a room in two years where nobody was checking.
He is genuinely clever and genuinely kind and has no idea what he wants, which is a much
lonelier problem than it sounds at seventeen.
**His structural problem:** he is a family project. Not villainous parents — proud, loving,
completely certain ones, with a plan that has him leaving this ward permanently.

**THE INVERSION (the series' whole architecture):**
She is invisible at school and sovereign in the shop. He is sovereign at school and invisible
in the chair. Each one is the other's opposite, and each is only real in the place the other
one lives. The shop is where both of them are true at the same time.

## THE ENGINE

- **The chair.** He comes back. Three weeks, then two, then more often than any haircut can
  justify, and neither of them says so. The appointment book is the romance's progress bar:
  his name in her handwriting, the interval shrinking, then a week where she just writes the
  time and not the name because she knows.
- **The secret.** Her cutting him is illegal. That is not a plot bomb waiting to explode; it is
  the thing that makes the shop a room only the two of them are in. They both understand the
  risk precisely and choose it anyway, weekly, with scissors.
- **The silence at school.** Neither tells anyone they know the other. NOT from shame — the
  reason is stated plainly by ch6 and never wavers: the shop is the one place either of them
  is real, and school would contaminate it. When it does come out, that is the arc-1 finale.
- **The tsumuji.** She sees the crown of his head every time — the one part of himself he will
  never see. Nobody has ever looked at him that closely without wanting something. Recurs,
  unremarked, and pays at 55.

**ROMANCE SHAPE (Horimiya-register, locked):** they are not held apart for 55 chapters by a
contrivance. The confession lands in the **first third (~ch16)** and the series continues as
the real thing: two people who now have each other, an ensemble that opens up around them, the
shop, the licence, the third year, and the question of what happens to a person who was always
going to leave. **The back forty chapters are the relationship, not the wait.**

## SETTING (canon)

**HANAZONO-CHŌ**, an ordinary outer-ward neighbourhood: a private rail line, four station
exits with the poster boards, a shopping street of about forty shops, a river path, a high
school twenty minutes' walk from all of it.

**BARBER KOMORI (理容コモリ)** — two chairs, a striped pole with a cracked casing that has
turned at the wrong speed since 2011, a shampoo basin, hot-towel cabinet, a wall of framed
licences, an appointment book written in pencil, a til that sticks. Living quarters above.
**The second chair has not been used since Nagi's grandmother stopped cutting** — it is
maintained perfectly and nobody sits in it. That chair is the series' final image.

**KŌYŌ HIGH SCHOOL** — ordinary, decent, uniformed, twenty minutes away. Where both of them
are someone else.

## ENSEMBLE (cast agent builds full sheets; roles locked)

1. **Nagi's father** — 48, barber, extraordinary with hair, hopeless with sentences; speaks in
   threes. Knows more than he says. His arc is ch23.
2. **Nagi's grandmother** — 74, retired barber, lives upstairs, sharp as a blade, taught Nagi
   everything, and is the only person who has ever seen both of Nagi's faces. ALIVE and stays
   alive; her retirement is arthritis and boredom, not tragedy.
3. **The classmate who sees** (ch12) — a girl who is genuinely nice, not a gossip, and whose
   knowing changes everything gently.
4. **Tōma's one real friend** — the single person at school who treats him as a person rather
   than a score, and who is funny about it.
5. **The oldest regular** — a man who has sat in chair one every third Saturday for forty
   years and calls Nagi by her grandmother's name half the time.
6. **The girl in love with the poster** — in love with an *image*, learns Tōma is a person,
   and becomes a friend. **She is NOT a romantic rival and never becomes one.**
7. **Tōma's parents** — proud, loving, certain, and wrong in a way neither is a villain for.

## RECURRING MOTIFS (seed early, pay late)

1. **The appointment book** — the interval shrinking, in pencil.
2. **The poster** — weathering across 31 chapters; a corner lifting; someone draws on it; it
   comes down at 31.
3. **The hot towel** — the shop's small ceremony; who wraps whom.
4. **Hair on the floor** — every chapter ends up swept; the sweeping is where they talk.
5. **The second chair** — never used, always in frame, final image.
6. **"How short?"** — her first line to every customer, including him, forever. At 55 it is
   asked by someone else.
7. **The pole** — cracked casing, wrong speed; nobody fixes it; at some point somebody does.

## VOICE

- **Nagi at school:** almost nothing. Short, flat, deflecting. She is good at not being asked.
- **Nagi in the shop:** fast, warm, blunt, funny, technical. Real.
- **Tōma in public:** immaculate manners, complete sentences, gives nothing away.
- **Tōma in the chair:** dry, honest, oddly funny, and increasingly unguarded — the only
  register where he asks questions instead of answering them.
- Nobody explains their feelings. They discuss the length, the weather, the till, the towel.
  If a line states an emotion, rewrite it as an instruction about an object.
- Every chapter ends on a pull. Titles only fully land after reading.

---

## BANNED

- No fantasy, magic, system, or supernatural layer of any kind.
- **No love triangles, ever.** No romantic rival at any point, from anyone.
- No tsundere denial constructs. No accidental gropes, no falling-into-kisses, no towel gags.
- No beach or hot-spring filler. School events are legal; **no fireworks confession, no
  festival confession, no rain confession, no station-platform sprint.**
- No amnesia, no terminal illness, no dead parent revealed for leverage. The grandmother lives.
- No villain. Not the father, not the parents, not the school, not the classmate who sees.
  Everyone is doing their honest best from where they stand.
- No monologue over 4 bubbles.
- No "I've never had anyone be kind to me before."
- The leads never misunderstand each other for longer than one page. External misreadings are
  legal; between those two there is only the unsaid, never the mistaken.
- No character is humiliated for a laugh. Nobody is the butt.

## SHOWRUNNER RULINGS (post-bible review — canon, binding on every writer)

- **Sub-four-word bubbles are legal in exactly two forms:** the signature line **"HOW SHORT?"**,
  and pure reaction beats, capped at 2 per page. Everything else obeys 4-8 words. The line is
  the series' heartbeat; it does not get padded.
- **Nagi's mother:** alive, remarried, two prefectures away, texts about the weather, on good
  terms with everyone. She is NEVER a plot point, never a wound, never a lever. Confirmed so
  that no writer invents her badly.
- **DOKI-type heartbeat SFX: capped at 3 in the series, ZERO scheduled.** No writer may spend
  one without an explicit showrunner instruction in that cycle's brief. The default substitute
  is the held silent beat panel.
- **The one acknowledged aside in the whole series fires at CH50** — someone answers an aside
  they were not meant to hear, on the chapter where the leaving is decided. Nowhere else, ever.
- **The one out-of-shop "HOW SHORT?" fires at CH44**, when she commits to the licence — the
  first time she is a barber out in the world instead of only in that room. (Ch55's "how
  short?" is asked by someone else, per motif 6, and is a different beat.)
- **The towel reversal fires at ~CH35:** he wraps the hot towel for her. Once, never repeated.
- **The cracked pole** is fixed by Hagiwara at ~CH47; its SFX permanently loses its stutter
  from that chapter on, and nobody remarks on it.
- **Tōma reverts to "Komori-san"** whenever he is about to raise leaving — approved as a
  standing tell, unexplained forever.
- **Nagi's single pre-16 slip** ("Tōma", once) lands at **CH14**, mid-chore, unremarked.
- **NAMES: `cast.md` is the single authority.** The two bibles were written in parallel and
  arc.md has been reconciled to cast.md. Canonical ensemble: father **KOMORI TAKAFUMI** (48);
  grandmother **KOMORI YAEKO / "Yae"** (74, alive at ch55); classmate **NUNOKAWA KOHARU**;
  Tōma's friend **YAGISAWA GAKU**; oldest regular **HAGIWARA KIYOSHI** (79, retired watch
  repairer, 41 years in chair one); poster girl **MOMOSE RURIKA**; Tōma's parents **NARUSE
  TSUTOMU** and **NARUSE AKIE**. No writer may introduce a variant spelling or a new name for
  an existing role.
- **CH55 FINAL PAGE (locked):** both chairs full and the shop open — **YAEKO in the second
  chair with NAGI behind it** (the locked final image: the chair empty for 54 chapters is
  finally occupied by the woman it belongs to), Takafumi at chair one with Tōma in it, and the
  series' last line is **Takafumi asking Tōma "HOW SHORT?"** — motif 6 paid by someone else.
- **Nagi's mother** is confirmed exactly as `cast.md` has her: alive, remarried, elsewhere,
  texts about the weather, never a plot point. arc.md's silence about her is correct; she is
  simply not a story engine.
