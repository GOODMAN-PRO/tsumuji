# TSUMUJI — STYLE & CRAFT

The writing law. Expands `bible/pillars.md`; obeys `engine/chapter-engine.md`.
Nothing here may contradict either. If a chapter fails a rule in this file, the chapter is
wrong, not the rule.

---

## 0. BUBBLE DISCIPLINE

- **2-8 words per bubble; eight is the HARD ceiling.** Most bubbles are 3-6 words. One-word reactions are timing marks, never speeches.
- **11-15 speech/thought bubbles per page. Default 12-14.** Splash pages: 4-7.
- **Maximum 4 bubbles in any one panel.** Four is reserved for a rapid interruption chain.
- **Density comes from COUNT, never from LENGTH.** Eight quick lines across four small panels is
  the texture. A long speech is a bubble chain, never a paragraph in an oval.
- **No monologue over 4 bubbles**, ever, from anyone, including thought bubbles.

### The short-beat forms

Sub-four-word bubbles are manga timing, and only two classes use them:

1. **"How short?"** — the series' signature line. Two words, its own bubble, never padded, never
   ironic, never combined with anything else in the same oval.
2. **Pure reaction beats** — "Mm.", "Yeah.", "No.", "Ah.", "Wait." — **maximum three per page.** They count
   toward the page's bubble total and they never carry the point of a panel.

Everything else is usually three to eight words; two-word interruptions are allowed when the cut needs them.

### THE LAW: NOBODY EXPLAINS THEIR FEELINGS

Not once. Not in a thought bubble, not at the emotional peak, not on the last page of an arc.
They discuss **the length, the water temperature, the till, the towel, the homework, the
interval, the floor.** The feeling is in the panel, the hands, the interval and the reader.
The words are about an object.

### THE REWRITE RULE (mechanical)

> **FEELING → OBJECT IN REACH → INSTRUCTION ABOUT THE OBJECT.**

1. Name the feeling privately. Never put that sentence on the page.
2. Find the nearest object either character's hands could reach **in this panel**.
3. Write an instruction, a measurement, or a complaint about that object, 4-8 words, **that is
   only true if the feeling is true.**

**Reverse-check:** delete the object from the bubble. If the line still works, the line is wrong.
**Deniability check:** the speaker must be able to shrug it off in the next panel. If they could
not, it is a confession — and confessions are rationed to roughly four in the whole series.

### FIVE WORKED EXAMPLES

| Feeling (never written) | Object in reach | The line that ships |
|---|---|---|
| *I missed you.* | the appointment book | "You let it go three weeks." |
| *I'm frightened you'll leave.* | the pencil | "Book it before you go home." |
| *I don't want this to end.* | the broom | "Don't sweep yet. It's fine there." |
| *I'm proud of you.* | the comb and the water | "Wet it. Comb it. Good." |
| *I'm not okay.* | the basin tap | "The water's fine. Leave it hot." |

## 1. WRITING QUIET INTIMACY ON THE PAGE

The section this series lives or dies on. A haircut is not a scene transition here; it is the
scene, and it has a fixed rhythm the reader learns by chapter three and then feels the absence
of whenever it is broken.

### THE SEVEN-BEAT HAIRCUT

A full cut runs **6-9 pages**. A compressed cut (beats 1, 5 and 7 only) runs **2-3 pages**. Do
not play all seven beats more than **twice per arc** or the ritual turns into wallpaper.

| # | Beat | Pages | What it is for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **CONSULTATION** | 1-1.5 | He is already in the chair; she comes in behind him. "How short?" Camera over the shoulder into the mirror. **The chapter's real subject gets stated here, as a question about length.** |
| 2 | **CAPE** | 0.5 | The large white shape arrives; the snap; the paper neck strip; **her fingers at the back of his neck.** This is the chapter's first contact, every time, without exception. |
| 3 | **WATER** | 1-1.5 | Spray bottle (ordinary) or **basin** (the loaded version). At the basin she is above him and neither can see the other's face — the only configuration in the series where somebody says a true thing by accident. **Cap: six basin scenes in fifty-five chapters.** |
| 4 | **THE CUT** | 2-3 | Where the talk happens. Small panels, fast bubbles, comb-and-shear inserts. Her voice at maximum, his questions at maximum. |
| 5 | **THE PAUSE** | 0.5-1 | The shears stop. 2-4 panels. **Triggered by something HE says, and she does not answer it** — she resumes work, and her hands answer instead. The beat panel usually lives here. |
| 6 | **THE TOWEL** | 0.5 | The shop's small ceremony. His eyes close: the only time his face is unguarded in plain view, and the only time she can look at it directly without cost. |
| 7 | **THE MIRROR** | 0.5-1 | The hand mirror held up to show him the back of his own head. **The tsumuji beat.** He cannot see it; she has been looking at it for twenty minutes. Chapters usually close here or over the broom. |

### WHEN TO CUT TO HANDS — the series' signature close-up

Cut to hands when: **(a)** a bubble would otherwise state a feeling — draw the hands instead and
delete the line; **(b)** the first contact of the chapter; **(c)** somebody is lying; **(d)** the
other person's face would be too much for the page to hold and still stay quiet.

**Budget: 3-6 hand inserts per chapter, never more than two consecutively.** Hands are drawn
**larger and with more line than faces** — this is the one place the house style spends detail.
Note this does not break the art spec's face law: hand inserts are the permitted
one-panel-without-a-person per page, and they are used at that rate deliberately.

### WHEN TO CUT TO THE MIRROR

The mirror is the only way these two look at each other without looking at each other. It is a
device, not a background, and it has rules:

- **Direct eye contact between the leads is RATIONED.** Maximum **3 direct-gaze panels per
  chapter before ch16**, and **exactly one in chapter 1.** Everything else routes through glass.
- **A lie is told to the direct face. The truth is told to the mirror.** No exceptions before 16.
- The reflection is drawn **cleaner** than the direct shot: fewer lines, no tone on clothing, no
  reflected room. And **Tōma's reflection carries a second eye highlight his direct shots never
  have** — the glass sees what the room does not.
- **When both of them are looking at the mirror in the same panel, that panel carries no
  dialogue.** Ever. That is the beat-panel trigger and it is automatic.

### BEAT PANELS — the held silence, and its notation

**Notation:** append `[BEAT]` to the panel tag, exactly as `[CHIBI]` is appended.
`PANEL 4 [small] [BEAT]:` — a beat panel carries **no speech bubble, no thought bubble, no
aside and no SFX.** Nothing. It is a full stop drawn as a rectangle.

- **1-3 beat panels per chapter.** Never two in a row. Never on page 1.
- The page containing one still carries its normal 11-15 bubbles from other panels.
  **THERE ARE NO SILENT PAGES. A beat is a panel, never a page.**
- A beat panel's content is always **one** of: hands, the two of them in the mirror, the crown of
  a head, the floor and its flecks, the second chair.
- **THE LAW: the most intimate panel in any chapter contains no dialogue at all.** If the biggest
  moment in your chapter has a bubble in it, you have written the wrong bubble. Cut it and let
  the panel carry it. This is the single rule most responsible for how the book feels.
- A beat panel is the series' preferred substitute for a heartbeat SFX. Always. See §3.

### DRAWING TWO PEOPLE GETTING CLOSER WITHOUT TOUCHING

The trap in a barbershop romance is that professional touch is **constant from page one** — her
hands are in his hair for twenty minutes a chapter. So touch is not the ladder. **The ladder is
body distance, and whether her hands are working when they touch him.**

**The ruler is her forearm.** Give the artist the measurement, not the mood:

| Ch band | Distance | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| 1-5 | a full arm's length | elbow locked, body squared to the chair back, feet planted apart |
| 6-11 | half an arm | the elbow bends; **her hip touches the arm of the chair** — the chair is touched, never him |
| 12-15 | a forearm | she braces two fingers on the crown to steady his head: the first non-work touch that is still technically work |
| 16-22 | none | she stops squaring to the chair and stands **beside** it; they share the mirror's frame |
| 23+ | the distance stops being the subject | proximity becomes ordinary, and the ache moves to the calendar |

**NON-WORKING TOUCH IS COUNTED.** Before ch16 there are **exactly four** touches between the
leads where her hands are not doing a job. Four. The arc agent places them and logs each one in
`continuity/log.md`. A fifth is a continuity error, not a nice moment.

**Other tools for closing distance without contact:** the shared object (a broom handed over, a
towel, the appointment book turned round); the shrinking gap between two heads in the mirror
frame across a chapter; the panel that used to show both of them and now crops to one plus a
piece of the other; and the interval in the book, which is the loudest of the lot and is
entirely made of pencil.

## 2. THE ADDRESS LADDER

The romance's progress bar. It is pinned to chapters, it only ever moves forward, and
**every rung change happens mid-chore.**

> **ENFORCEMENT TEST:** if the panel where a name changes has nobody handling an object, move
> the panel. A rung that lands in a dramatic scene is a rung written wrong.

### THE TWO OF THEM

| Ch | She calls him | He calls her | Mechanic |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **nothing** — "you", "head down", "chin" | **"Komori-san"** | She addresses the head, not the person. The reader clocks that she never says his name. |
| 2-3 | **"Naruse-san"** | "Komori-san" | She reads it off the appointment page, out loud, in his hearing. |
| 4-11 | **"Naruse"** | "Komori-san" | Suffix dropped — a barber's habit with a regular. First rung, and it happens **while she is writing in the book.** |
| 6 | *at school: nothing* | *at school: nothing* | The rule is agreed over sweeping. Neither uses the other's name inside the building, and this holds until the arc-1 finale. |
| 12 | (unchanged) | (unchanged) | Koharu sees. **Deliberately nothing moves.** The ladder ignores plot. |
| **14** | **"Tōma."** — ONCE | — | **THE SLIP.** Mid-instruction, hands busy, no eye contact. Neither of them acknowledges it. She does not do it again until 16. |
| 16 | **"Tōma"** | still **"Komori-san"** | They get together and **he does not change what he calls her.** It is what he has always called her; it has quietly become an endearment. |
| 20 | "Tōma" | **"Nagi"** — first use | Mid-chore, carrying a bin bag out to the alley, not looking at her. **A bigger event than the confession, and the chapter treats it as smaller.** |
| 21-37 | "Tōma" | "Nagi" | Settled. |
| 38-54 | "Tōma" | "Nagi" — **except** | **THE ALARM: when he is about to say something about leaving, he reverts to "Komori-san."** The reader learns to dread two words. |
| 55 | — | — | Finale. The last address in the series is not between them: it is somebody in the second chair being asked "How short?" |

### THE ENSEMBLE

| Who | Calls | What | Mechanic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Takafumi | Nagi | **"Nagi."** | No suffix, no nickname, fifty-five chapters. |
| Takafumi | Tōma | "the boy" → "Naruse-kun" → **"Tōma-kun"** | The third form arrives after ch23. That is how you know 23 landed. |
| Yae | Nagi | **"Nagi-chan"** / **"Komori"** | Surname, flat, means *I am speaking to you as a barber now.* Everyone straightens up. |
| Hagiwara | Nagi | **"Yae-chan" in the mirror; "Nagi-chan" face to face.** | Exact and mechanical. In the glass, over a white cape, she is Yae at twenty. Nobody corrects him — Yae forbade it. |
| Koharu | Nagi | "Komori-san" → "Komori" → "Nagi-chan" (~ch30) | Nagi **never** returns the escalation. She says "Nunokawa" for fifty-five chapters and means the world by it. |
| Gaku | Tōma | **"Naruse."** Always. | He never once says "Tōma", and that is the friendship: he does not need the access. |

| Akie & Tsutomu | Tōma | "Tōma" | Both, always. Except once, late, when Tsutomu says **"Tōma-kun"** — the moment he starts addressing his son as an adult, which is to say as a stranger. |

## 3. SFX LEXICON

25 entries. Romaji, hand-lettered, with the English in parentheses beneath, exactly as the art
spec requires. **2-5 SFX per page.** This book's sounds are small and domestic far more often
than they are large.

### THE SHOP

| SFX | English | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **SHAKI** | (snip) | One clean shear close. **The series' signature sound** — used more than any other SFX in the book. Always small. |
| **SHAKI-SHAKI** | (snip-snip) | A fast run of point-cuts. Her hands at working speed. |
| **KACHIN** | (clack) | Shears set down on the glass shelf. **This sound ends a beat** — it is a full stop. |

### BODY / FEELING — read this section twice

| SFX | English | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **SU** | (a breath in) | The smallest sound in the book, and the preferred one. Use it wherever a lesser manga would reach for a heartbeat. |
| **KAAA** | (flush) | Heat in the ears and neck. **Maximum once per five chapters** — the art spec's cheek hatching does this job most of the time. |

> **THE SUBSTITUTE, AND IT IS THE HOUSE PREFERENCE:** where a heartbeat would go, use a **held
> silent beat panel** — `PANEL k [size] [BEAT]` — no bubble, no aside, no SFX, nothing. The page
> keeps its 11-15 bubbles elsewhere. A drawn silence beats a drawn heartbeat every single time,
> and this series has fifty-five chapters to prove it.

## 4. RECURRING MOTIFS

The seven from `pillars.md`, expanded, plus three new object motifs. Every one is an **object**.
None is ever built on humiliation. Frequency caps are hard: a motif over its cap is a continuity
error and the lint of the eye catches it faster than any reader admits.

**1. THE APPOINTMENT BOOK**
*Seed:* ch1 — he goes in the margin as a walk-in, in pencil, five minutes before closing.
*Repetition:* drawn open on the current spread, her small upright handwriting, and shown **only
when the interval changes.** The interval is the romance's progress bar: three weeks, then two,
then more often than any haircut can justify, and neither of them ever says so.
*Cap:* **max 2 panels per chapter, and no more than one chapter in three may show it at all.**
Shown every chapter, it stops meaning anything.
*Payoff:* **~ch16 — the week she writes only the time and not the name, because she knows.**
Second payoff ~ch50: a page ahead of the current one with nothing written on it. At 55 the book
has two columns, because there are two chairs.

**2. THE POSTER**
*Seed:* ch1 — outside the window, behind the pole, and **she never once looks at it.**
*Repetition:* the weathering ladder in `cast.md`, one state per chapter band. Always cropped,
angled or occluded. Always the coarse dot screen.
*Cap:* **max 2 panels per chapter**, and **no more than five spoken references to its condition
across the whole of chapters 1-31.** The rain-run streak from the eye is never named by anybody.
*Payoff:* **ch31 — it comes down.** Rurika takes it home. After 31, the ghost rectangle appears
occasionally and **carries no dialogue about it, ever.**

**3. THE HOT TOWEL**
*Seed:* ch1 — she wraps him; it is simply the service.
*Repetition:* who wraps whom. His eyes close; it is the only time his face is unguarded in plain
view.
*Cap:* **one towel beat per chapter maximum — and it may be skipped.** A chapter where she does
not do the towel is louder than a chapter where she does. Use that.
*Payoff:* **the first time he wraps her** (proposed ~ch35: she falls asleep in chair one after a
long day, he does it wrong, she lets him).

**4. HAIR ON THE FLOOR**
*Seed:* ch1.
*Repetition:* **every chapter.** The floor is a clock inside the scene — clean, drifted, swept.
The sweeping is where they talk, because sweeping is the only chore that faces away.
*Cap:* **no more than two consecutive chapters may end on the broom.** Break the pattern or the
pattern breaks the book.
*Payoff:* ch55, somebody else sweeping. Mid-series payoff: **the chapter he takes the broom out
of her hands and she lets him** — a rung, unremarked.

**5. THE SECOND CHAIR**
*Seed:* ch1 — in frame, empty, immaculate.
*Repetition:* **in frame in at least one panel of every shop scene.** Nobody sits in it. Anything
set down on it is removed before the scene ends — that is the artist's continuity check.
*Cap:* **zero dialogue about it before ch23. At most one line about it in any chapter after.**
*Payoff:* **ch55. The last image of the series.**

**6. "HOW SHORT?"**
*Seed:* ch1, to him, five minutes before closing.
*Repetition:* her first line to every customer who has ever sat down. **Said to Tōma in every
cutting chapter — and his answer changes, and his answer is the chapter's temperature.**
*Cap:* never ironic, never a joke, and **never said by Nagi outside the shop — except once.**
That once is reserved; showrunner schedules it.
*Payoff:* **ch55 — somebody else asks it.**

**7. THE POLE**
*Seed:* ch1 — cracked casing, wrong speed since 2011.
*Repetition:* the stutter SFX, once per chapter maximum; drawn cropped or through the window,
never centre-frame. **Three separate characters offer to fix it and are refused** (Takafumi's
refusal is a three-beat).
*Cap:* the crack is drawn in the same place every single time. One pole panel per chapter.
*Payoff:* **Hagiwara fixes it** (proposed ch47-52) — shaking hands, no speech, and the SFX loses
its stutter for the rest of the series. Nobody makes anything of it and everybody notices.

### THREE NEW MOTIFS

**8. THE TWO STEEL PINS**
*Seed:* ch2 — she takes them out of her hair and holds them **crossed between her teeth** while
she re-ties it. When she is thinking hard she puts one there with no hair to pin.
*Repetition:* every state-switch, and every time she is working a problem.
*Cap:* **twice per chapter.**
*Payoff, proposed:* she hands him one to hold — a stupid, tiny, enormous act performed
mid-chore, with no line attached. He still has it much later. It is the one object he takes.

**9. THE TILL THAT STICKS**
*Seed:* ch1 — GAKON, and Takafumi closes it with his hip without looking.
*Repetition:* the hip-bump is a family gesture; Nagi does it, her father does it, Yae does it
from a seated position and it works. Nobody has ever explained it to anybody.
*Cap:* **once per chapter, and only when somebody is already at the counter.**
*Payoff, proposed (~ch20s):* **Tōma does it without thinking.** No dialogue, no reaction shot
longer than one panel. That is the chapter he belongs to the shop, and nobody says a word.

**10. THE PAPER NECK STRIP**
*Seed:* ch1 — the thin paper collar tucked under the cape, the most intimate legal touch in the
trade, and it happens before every single cut in the series.
*Repetition:* **never skipped, never remarked on.** It is always the chapter's first contact, it
is always drawn, and the reader comes to wait for it without knowing they are waiting.
*Cap:* 1-2 panels, one appearance per cut.
*Payoff:* two of them. **(a)** The once, late, that somebody finally says something about it.
**(b)** Better and earlier: the chapter where **his collar is already turned down before she
reaches for it**, because he has learned the sequence — drawn, uncommented, one panel.

## 5. MUTTERED ASIDES

Small unbubbled text floating beside a head (or in a tiny dashed bubble). **No bubble. Six words
maximum. Always true. Never acknowledged.**

### TWO CLASSES — read this before counting anything

The engine budgets **3-5 asides per page**. That budget is filled by **working mutters**, not by
the loaded kind. They are different instruments:

- **WORKING MUTTERS — uncapped.** Counting, correcting, reading the till, cursing the pole,
  narrating a chore. Texture. This is the series' comedy engine and it runs all day.
- **TRUE ASIDES — capped at THREE per chapter per character.** The ones that are *about the
  other person*. Always true, never acknowledged, never repeated, and they are the reason the
  form exists. Spend them like the DOKI budget.

**THE ONE EXCEPTION:** in fifty-five chapters, **exactly one true aside is heard and answered**
by the person it is about. One. It is reserved. *Showrunner schedules it* — the strongest
available slot is Nagi's "grow faster," and the answer is one word, not a conversation.

### THE REGISTER SPLIT — the two leads' asides can never be swapped

| | Nagi | Tōma |
|---|---|---|
| **Grammar** | **imperatives and measurements** | **questions and conditionals** |
| **Never** | never a question, never a hedge | never an imperative, never a number given as fact |
| **Mechanical check** | **no subject** — the sentence starts at the verb or the number | **no certainty** — it ends in a hedge, a query, or an "or" |
| **Shape** | "two mil. off the left." | "...three weeks. or two." |

**Why this split and no other:** her whole voice is **instructions aimed at a head** — her mind
is a hand adjusting something, so her asides are the correction reflex firing with no object in
front of it. His public register is defined by a hard law — **no contractions and no questions**
— so his asides are the only place the questions get out. **His asides are the pressure valve on
his public voice.** Give her a question or him an order and the character has been swapped, and
any reader who has got to chapter ten will feel it before they can say why.

### EIGHT EXAMPLES

**Nagi:**
- two mil. off the left.
- don't nod. don't nod.
- left ear's lower. always.
- grow faster.

**Tōma:**
- ...or is that rude.
- three weeks. or two.
- how does she know.
- if she meant that.

## 6. BANNED

`pillars.md`'s list, folded together with this file's. Any one of these is a redraft, not a note.

**World and genre**
- No fantasy, magic, system, or supernatural layer of any kind.
- No beach or hot-spring filler. School events are legal; **no fireworks confession, no festival
  confession, no rain confession, no station-platform sprint.**
- No amnesia. No terminal illness. No dead parent revealed for leverage. **The grandmother lives.**

**Romance**
- **No love triangles, ever. No romantic rival at any point, from anyone.** Momose Rurika is not
  one and never becomes one.
- No tsundere denial constructs. No accidental gropes, no falling-into-kisses, no towel gags.
- The leads never misunderstand each other for longer than one page. External misreadings are
  legal; between those two there is only the unsaid, never the mistaken.

**People**
- **No villain.** Not the father, not the parents, not the school, not the classmate who sees.
  Everyone is doing their honest best from where they stand.
- **No gag built on humiliation. Nobody is the butt.**
- No "I've never had anyone be kind to me before," or any relative of that sentence.

**Page and line**
- **No bubble over 8 words.** No monologue over 4 bubbles.
- **No character stating an emotion in dialogue.** Rewrite it as an instruction about an object.
- **No silent pages.** Every page carries dialogue, page 1 included. A beat is a panel.
- No DOKI-type heartbeat SFX beyond three in the series, and never on a confession page.
- No stock line. If the sentence could appear in any other manga, it does not go in this one.

## 7. CHAPTER CHECKLIST

Nine boxes. A breakdown that cannot tick all nine goes back to the writer before any drawing
starts, whatever the lint script says.

- [ ] **1. Opens inside somebody's real life** — a chore, a conversation already running, a hand
      already doing something. **Never on an establishing shot of scenery.** Page 1 carries the
      caption *and* dialogue.
- [ ] **2. A chore or craft beat with real page time** — not a cutaway. Food, laundry, a
      bandage, a bus ride, the till, the floor, a head. It gets panels, not a panel.
- [ ] **3. Both leads' true faces are visible at least once** — hers with the whorl covered and
      her sleeves rolled, his with the tie down one finger-width and a question in his mouth.
- [ ] **4. One correct money-or-craft detail** — a price, a licence rule, a shear technique, a
      growth rate, a rota, an appointment interval. **One per chapter, verifiable, unexplained.**
      The reader should feel the trade is real without being taught anything.
- [ ] **5. A motif run** — at least one motif from §4, inside its cap, doing work rather than
      waving.
- [ ] **6. Warmth before ache** — the chapter is funny and warm first and the ache arrives
      underneath it. Never the reverse. **Comedy is texture, never the engine.**
- [ ] **7. Outside-beat count is 0 or 1, and it pays a character** — at most one external
      development (exam, parent, customer, school event, deadline), and it must land on somebody
      rather than move a plot.
- [ ] **8. Address ladder respected** — every name used matches §2 for this chapter number, and
      any rung change happens mid-chore with an object in somebody's hands.
- [ ] **9. Chapter-end pull, and a title that only lands after reading** — the last page turns
      the reader forward, and the title on page 1 means something different on the last page
      than it did on the first.
