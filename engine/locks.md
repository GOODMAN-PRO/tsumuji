WORLD RENDER RULES (apply on every page):
- NEUTRAL FULL-BODY BASELINE: the cast is generally tall and light, with narrow shoulders and long limbs. Character head-counts below apply ONLY to a neutral standing model-sheet view. They are identity guidance, not panel-by-panel measurements. Manga acting may compress, stretch, crop, foreshorten, crouch, slouch, enlarge a reaction head, or simplify a body. Do not redesign a character as broadly stocky in an ordinary standing shot, but never sacrifice a lively panel to perfect proportions.
- SCHOOL UNIFORM (KŌYŌ HIGH): single-breasted notch-lapel blazer, ONE button fastened. Blazer/trousers/skirt = flat 30% dot screentone, never solid black. Shirt/blouse = PURE WHITE PAPER, zero tone. Boys' necktie and girls' ribbon = FLAT SOLID BLACK (the only black accent above the waist). Skirt is a plain box pleat with fine white pleat lines; trousers straight, no cuff. Indoor shoes white with a toe band: SOLID BLACK = second year, untoned white = first year. NO crest, badge, or lettering anywhere on the uniform — the school's only insignia is a FINE WHITE PIPING LINE along the outer edge of each blazer cuff; draw it. Summer: blazer off, white short-sleeve shirt, tie/ribbon still worn.
- THE BARBERSHOP (BARBER KOMORI) — rendering canon: a mirror wall behind chair one; TWO chrome-and-leather barber chairs (chair one working, chair two the grandmother's and never occupied — keep it in frame, empty, perfect); a shampoo basin; a hot-towel cabinet; a wall of framed licences with ONE EMPTY FRAME at the end of the row; a pencil appointment book; a till. The CUTTING CAPE is a large PURE WHITE shape with zero tone — the biggest white area on any shop page. CUT HAIR ON THE FLOOR is fine scattered ink flecks, never clumps. Outside: a striped pole whose casing has a fine crack, turning at a slightly wrong speed.
- THE MIRROR IS A STORY DEVICE: the mirror is how these two look at each other without looking directly. Reflections are selectively drawn and often cropped; use fewer lines than the direct figure, omit unimportant room detail, and never render a complete ray-traced duplicate of the shop.
- THE POSTER (Naruse Tōma's cram-school portrait, on the utility pole outside the shop window and at four station exits): a large printed FACE rendered in a COARSER DOT SCREEN than the drawn world, so it reads as printed matter and not as a person. It is NEVER shown clean or whole — always cropped by the panel edge, angled, rain-streaked, or occluded by something in front of it. It carries NO legible text. It weathers steadily across the series.
- SIGNAGE LAW — NEVER INVENT LETTERING. Any sign, poster, shopfront, notice, book, packet or screen may ONLY carry text the PAGE SCRIPT explicitly supplies. If the script gives no wording, draw the lettering illegible — turned away, blurred, edge-on, cropped. Never invent a name.
- Page values change with the beat: the book is generally light, but one page may be mostly white and the next may use heavy hair blacks, a dark emotion field, or more screentone. Do not enforce one white percentage across the chapter.

CHARACTER LOCKS:

KOMORI NAGI (lead, 17) — 168cm, drawn at 7.75 HEADS: small head, long slim neck, narrow gently sloping shoulders, long forearms, legs slightly more than half of total height, flat tapering torso. Never compact, never square-shouldered, never low-centred. HER HANDS ARE THE MOST CAREFULLY DRAWN FEATURE IN THE SERIES: long fingers, visible knuckles and tendon lines, short unpolished nails. HAIR: flat SOLID BLACK, blunt and heavy, always worn UP — she is the black shape in every two-shot. EYES: narrow-set and level. CLOTHES at school: blazer worn OPEN always, ribbon a half-turn off-centre, shirt cuffs BUTTONED, single bag strap hooked on the elbow (never both shoulders — her hands stay free), scuffed toe on one white shoe. In the shop: a barber's straight-cut bib apron in FLAT SOLID BLACK over her school blouse, ties crossed at the back and knotted in front, sleeves rolled TWICE above the elbow with bare forearms drawn with a tendon line, flat black lace-ups. PROPS: a scissor sheath on her LEFT HIP holding three pairs handles-up, one pair with WHITE TAPE wound round the finger ring (that pair is hers); a wide-tooth comb; two steel bobby pins.
**NAGI'S STATE-SWITCH — the single most important rule in this file, every panel, no exceptions:** AT SCHOOL her hair is scraped up so hard that HER OWN CROWN WHORL IS EXPOSED on top of her head — twisted into a flat coil pinned at the nape with TWO STEEL PINS CROSSED IN AN X, hairline showing, silhouette small and closed. Her eyes: upper lash line only, ONE small highlight low in the iris, half-lidded. IN THE SHOP the pins are out, the hair is re-tied HIGH AND LOOSE with the comb driven through it, volume over the crown, THE WHORL COVERED, and ONE LONG STRAND loose on her left. Her eyes gain a fine LOWER-LID STROKE and a SECOND pinpoint highlight lower-right, and open a millimetre wider. **WHORL DRAWN = school. WHORL HIDDEN = shop.** Redundant tell: cuffs buttoned at school, rolled twice in the shop.

NARUSE TŌMA (lead, 17) — 176cm, drawn at 8 HEADS: small head, long neck, narrow sloping shoulders that he holds level and still, long arms, long legs, slim flat torso. Never broad, never blocky, never square. HAIR: LIGHT — 20% dot screentone with a wide clean white shine band — against Nagi's solid black, so any two-shot separates instantly. His crown whorl sits LEFT OF CENTRE and lifts; it is the reason his hair has never sat right. NAPE LAW (the series' clock): a SQUARE-CUT nape means the chain salon and the poster; a SOFT POINTED nape with needle sideburns and a fringe broken into fine points means Nagi cut it — any panel can be dated from the back of his head. EYES: long straight upper lashes, heavy lash line meeting the iris, irises in fine vertical striations, EXACTLY ONE SMALL SQUARE HIGHLIGHT, always — EXCEPT IN MIRROR REFLECTIONS, where a second tiny highlight appears. CLOTHES: blazer buttoned, top shirt button done, tie knot tight and DRAWN THE SAME SIZE IN EVERY PANEL, cuffs at the wrist bone, bag carried in the hand never slung. Out of school: plain PURE WHITE crew-neck knit over a shirt, straight 30%-tone trousers, plain low sneakers — and NOTHING HE OWNS CARRIES LETTERING, ever. PROPS: a mechanical pencil worn shiny with the clip snapped off; a furred over-folded timetable card. TRUE-FACE TELL: in the chair the top button opens and the tie knot drops EXACTLY ONE FINGER-WIDTH.

KOMORI TAKAFUMI (48, Nagi's father, the barber) — 178cm, 7.75 HEADS, long-boned and narrow, sloping shoulders, long forearms, a slight forward set to the neck from thirty years standing over a chair. Never bulky, never squat, never square. HAIR: 20% tone, white at the temples only (leave the temples as WHITE PAPER), clipped so short at the sides it reads as texture rather than shape; his own crown is thinning and he cuts around it exactly the way Nagi cuts around Tōma's. EYES: heavy straight lash line, small irises, ONE highlight, lids sitting low — he always looks like a man about to say something who then does not. CLOTHES: a white barber's tunic buttoned at the shoulder (PURE WHITE PAPER, zero tone), sleeves to the elbow, A COMB IN THE BREAST POCKET IN EVERY SINGLE PANEL. PROP: a straight razor he strops every morning and no longer uses on strangers.

KOMORI YAEKO (74, grandmother, retired barber, lives upstairs) — **SHE MUST READ AT A GLANCE AS A WOMAN IN HER MID-SEVENTIES. If she could be mistaken for a young person or for a man, the panel is wrong.** 170cm and STILL TALL: 7.5 HEADS, long spine, long neck, narrow shoulders DROPPED rather than hunched, long thin hands with prominent knuckles and two crooked finger joints. NEVER draw her small, round, bent or squat — she is the tallest woman in the series and stands like somebody who spent fifty years not sitting down. AGE CUES, all required and all line-work (never shading): fine creases fanning at the outer eye corners and bracketing the mouth, HEAVY UPPER LIDS that hood the eye, a softened hollow under the cheekbone, a slightly slackened jawline, a visible tendon line at the throat, and thin skin over the hands. Her hair is thinning enough that the scalp shows faintly at the crown. HAIR: 10% tone (near white) with fine strand lines, a short crop she cuts herself, fringe visibly and deliberately uneven. EYES: single fine lash line, small dark irises, ONE highlight, outer corners LIFTED so she appears amused in every panel including the ones where she is not. CLOTHES: cardigan over a plain blouse, 30% tone, SLEEVES PUSHED UP EXACTLY LIKE HER GRANDDAUGHTER'S. PROP: a comb she carries everywhere and never uses.

All numeric head counts, exact tie sizes, exact highlight counts, and phrases such as `always`, `binding`, `every panel`, or `never compact` describe the neutral identity model only.
